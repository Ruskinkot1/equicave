# Controlled ablations in ligand binding-site prediction: what the evidence actually shows (as of 2026-10)

**Verification key used throughout:**
- **[P]** = primary, I downloaded the PDF and read the table/figure text myself.
- **[P-auto]** = primary source fetched, but the table was extracted by an automated page-reader rather than read by me line-by-line. Numbers should be spot-checked before quoting in a paper.
- **[2nd]** = second-hand (search snippet, abstract only, or summary of a source I did not read in full).

**Papers I read in full text [P]:** GDEGAN (arXiv 2603.19817), VN-EGNN (arXiv 2404.07194), EquiPocket (arXiv 2302.12177v3), NequIP (arXiv 2101.03164), "Beyond Canonicalization: How Tensorial Messages Improve Equivariant Message Passing" (arXiv 2405.15389, ICLR 2025), DeepSurf (arXiv 2002.05643).

---

## Key question 1: Which components have real controlled evidence, and which rest on a claim in an abstract?

### Takeaway

Only three components in this literature have an ablation where one factor is varied with everything else held fixed: protein-language-model node features, the auxiliary directional loss, and network depth (all in GDEGAN/VN-EGNN/EquiPocket) — plus DeepSurf's surface-grid alignment. The two most prominently *claimed* components, **equivariance-versus-invariance** and **virtual nodes**, have **no isolating ablation anywhere in the binding-site literature**; the headline comparisons are against results copied from other papers with different features, codebases and output heads. Spherical degree (lmax) is **never swept in any binding-site paper** — every one of them fixes it (GDEGAN at Lmax=2) and the only controlled lmax evidence comes from the force-field literature.

### Cited findings

#### Protein language model embeddings (ESM-2) — STRONGEST controlled evidence, and the largest single effect

- **[P] GDEGAN Table 2** is a clean one-factor swap. Holding GotenNet fixed and replacing atomic-number embeddings with ESM-2 embeddings (no auxiliary loss in either arm): COACH420 DCC **0.454(0.007) → 0.543(0.008)** (+0.089); HOLO4K DCC **0.464(0.001) → 0.520(0.011)** (+0.056); PDBbind2020 DCC **0.553(0.008) → 0.637(0.005)** (+0.084). The paper reports this as "**15.61% DCC and 9.27% DCA averaged across datasets**." — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] The ESM gain is feature-dependent (sub-additive).** In the same GDEGAN Table 2, when the auxiliary directional loss (ADL) is already present, adding ESM gives COACH420 +0.071 (0.485→0.556), HOLO4K +0.061 (0.468→0.529), PDBbind +0.057 (0.592→0.649) — i.e. the COACH420 and PDBbind gains shrink by ~20% and ~32% respectively relative to the no-ADL arms. — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] VN-EGNN Table 2 runs an independent and tighter ESM ablation and gets a *qualitatively different* DCA result.** Comparing the two `VN-EGNN (homog.)` rows, which differ only in ESM: COACH420 DCC 0.497(0.014) → 0.575(0.008) (+0.078) but DCA **0.700(0.013) → 0.708(0.009), i.e. +0.008, well inside the reported spread**; HOLO4K DCC 0.414(0.023) → 0.479(0.012) (+0.065) but DCA **0.618(0.024) → 0.595(0.010), ESM makes DCA WORSE by 0.023**; PDBbind2020 DCC 0.502(0.029) → 0.649(0.010) (+0.147), DCA 0.717(0.025) → 0.805(0.006) (+0.088). — [VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07194)
- **[P] The two ESM ablations differ in what the non-ESM arm contains**, which plausibly explains the disagreement. VN-EGNN's non-ESM arms use **learned residue-type embeddings** at Cα resolution (the rows are literally labelled "residue emb."), so its contrast is *ESM-2 vs. a learned residue identity embedding*. GDEGAN's non-ESM arm uses **atomic-number embeddings** ("Using ESM embeddings (GotenNet+ESM) instead of atomic numbers embeddings (GotenNet)"), a much more impoverished baseline. — [VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07194); [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] ESM is also the enabling condition for VN-EGNN's heterogeneous message passing.** Without ESM, heterogeneous MP is null-to-harmful (COACH420 DCC 0.497→0.503, +0.006, inside the 0.014–0.022 spread; COACH420 DCA 0.700→0.684, **−0.016**; HOLO4K DCA 0.618→0.605, **−0.013**). With ESM it clearly pays (COACH420 DCC +0.030, DCA +0.042; HOLO4K DCC +0.053, DCA +0.064). — [VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07194)
- **[P] GDEGAN's main table (Table 1)** shows the scale of the ESM effect relative to everything else: pure topological graphs (GAT 0.03M, GCN 0.06M, GCN2 0.11M) sit at COACH420 DCC 0.039–0.049, EGNN at 0.156(0.017), SchNet at 0.168(0.019), EquiPocket at 0.423(0.014), GotenNet (no ESM) at 0.464(0.007), GDEGAN (ESM) at 0.580(0.008). — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)

#### Equivariance vs. invariance — NO isolating ablation in any binding-site paper

- **[P] GDEGAN's "EQ" column is not an equivariance ablation.** Table 2's equivariance column changes from E(3) to SE(3) **only because adding ESM-2 features breaks reflection symmetry** (sequence information is chirality-bearing); the footnote and Appendix proof confirm ESM embeddings enter as invariant scalars and the architecture stays equivariant in both arms. Every arm in GDEGAN's ablation is equivariant; there is **no invariant arm at all**. The paper's own words: "SE(3) occurs at the input level through ESM embeddings." — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] EquiPocket does not ablate equivariance either.** Its baselines GCN/GAT/GCN2 are topological (no coordinates at all — "they only consider atom and chemical bond information, ignoring the spatial structure"), and SchNet is distance-only invariant. All of these are different codebases/feature sets, not an equivariance arm of EquiPocket. EquiPocket itself describes its global module as "E(3) equivariance/invariance" without separating the two. — [EquiPocket, arXiv 2302.12177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12177)
- **[P] VN-EGNN's only non-equivariant/non-VN comparator is EGNN with numbers copied from the EquiPocket paper** (Table 2 footnote b: "Results from Zhang et al. (2023b)"), i.e. a different implementation, different features (atomic, no ESM) and a different output head (per-atom classification + mean-shift vs. virtual-node coordinate regression). — [VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07194)
- **[P] The one paper that scalarises the SAME geometry in a local frame so that only the mechanism differs is "Beyond Canonicalization" (ICLR 2025)** — not a binding-site paper, but exactly the design the question asks for. It builds local frames from the same neighbourhood geometry and then either projects everything onto the frame (invariant *scalar* messages) or passes tensors. Normal-vector regression on ModelNet40, PointNet++ backbone, no frame refinement, 2×2 ablation (Table 3, cosine similarity, higher better):
  | | tensorial messages | scalar messages |
  |---|---|---|
  | learned local frames | **0.87** | 0.82 |
  | random local frames | **0.84** | 0.80 |
  The mechanism (tensor vs. scalar) is worth +0.05 with learned frames and +0.04 with random frames; frame informativeness is worth only +0.03/+0.02. The paper's conclusion: "the model with tensor messages significantly outperforms the model with scalar messages (even if the local frames are chosen randomly)." — [Beyond Canonicalization, arXiv 2405.15389](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15389)
- **[P] In that same paper the output head is held fixed across arms**, and the model-level comparison against plain data augmentation flips sign depending on the test-rotation distribution (Table 1): with aligned train/test (z/z) **data augmentation wins, 0.89 vs. 0.88** for the best equivariant model; under full O(3)/O(3) the equivariant model wins, **0.88 vs. 0.86**; under train-z/test-O(3) augmentation collapses to 0.79 vs. 0.87–0.88. — [Beyond Canonicalization, arXiv 2405.15389](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15389)
- **[P] The size of the scalarisation penalty is strongly task-dependent in that paper.** Normal-vector regression (geometry must be propagated precisely): +0.05 cosine similarity. ShapeNet part segmentation: learned+scalar 78.8 → learned+tensor 79.7 mIoU (+0.9), and the authors themselves write "for point cloud segmentation the gain is less significant, indicating that geometric information, e.g. in the form of characteristic directions, may be less important." ModelNet40 classification: 86.7 → 88.0. ScanObjectNN: 71.9 → 79.8. — [Beyond Canonicalization, arXiv 2405.15389](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15389)
- **[P] NequIP contains the one genuinely *capacity-matched* equivariance ablation I found.** It explicitly states the goal: "we perform a series of experiments with the goal of isolating the effect of using equivariant convolutions… we explicitly turn on or off interactions of higher order than l = 0." On bulk water, with "identical hyperparameters" and training sets from 10 to 1,000 structures, "the equivariant networks with l ∈ 1, 2, 3 significantly outperform the invariant networks with l = 0 for all data set sizes." To control capacity, the Methods give the l=0 arm 4× more scalar features: "the l = 1 network had a feature configuration of 64x0o + 64x0e + 64x1o + 64x1e while the original l = 0 network used 64x0e and feature-controlled l = 0 network used 512x0e"; weight- and feature-controlled results are in Appendix B and "**Both show qualitatively the same effect**." — [NequIP, arXiv 2101.03164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03164)
- **[P] But NequIP's l=0 arm also removes geometry from the input**, which is precisely the confound flagged in the brief: the l=0 arm is "a conventional invariant GNN-IP, involving only invariant convolutions over scalar features **in a SchNet-style fashion**" — distances only, no angular information. The output head does not change (energy is a scalar in both arms; forces are autograd derivatives), so NequIP's confound is input-side, not head-side. — [NequIP, arXiv 2101.03164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03164)
- **[2nd] At scale the equivariance advantage becomes a compute-efficiency advantage rather than a ceiling advantage.** Brehmer et al., "Does equivariance matter at scale?" (TMLR 2025) on a rigid-body interaction benchmark: "Equivariance improves data efficiency, but training non-equivariant models with data augmentation can close this gap given sufficient epochs"; "Scaling with compute follows a power law, with equivariant models outperforming non-equivariant ones at each tested compute budget"; "The optimal allocation of a compute budget onto model size and training duration differs between equivariant and non-equivariant models." I read only the abstract — **no numerical scaling exponents or parameter-matching details verified**. — [arXiv 2410.23179](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23179)

#### Spherical / tensor degree (lmax) — ZERO binding-site evidence

- **[P] No binding-site paper sweeps lmax.** GDEGAN fixes it: "We use 4 layers with hidden dimension 128, **Lmax = 2** for steerable features," and Table 5's hyperparameter search space lists `Lmax` with the single value `2` — the sweep covers layers {3,4,5,6}, heads {4,8}, dropout {0.1,0.2,0.5} etc., but not degree. VN-EGNN's EGNN backbone is l=1-only by construction (scalar + one vector channel). EquiPocket's EGNN/surface-EGNN is likewise l=1-only. — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817); [VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07194); [EquiPocket, arXiv 2302.12177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12177)
- **[P] GDEGAN nevertheless makes a synergy claim about degree without measuring it**: "high-degree representations (l ∈ {1, 2}) encode how geometric information transforms under rotations, while GDA determines which neighbors are relevant… Neither alone captures binding site heterogeneity as effectively as their combination." **No arm in Table 2 varies l.** This is a claim in the discussion, not a measurement. — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] NequIP is the cleanest degree sweep in molecular ML, and it localises the payoff at l=0→1.** "We run a convergence scan on the rotation order l ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3} and find that increasing the tensor rank beyond l = 1 gives a consistent improvement. The significant improvement from l = 0 to l = 1 highlights the crucial role of equivariance in obtaining improved accuracy on this task." Critically, the *qualitative* change is confined to l=0→1: "Further increasing the rotation order l beyond l = 1 again **only shifts the learning curve and does not result in an additional change in log-log slope**." — [NequIP, arXiv 2101.03164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03164)
- **[P-auto] EquiformerV2 shows the sign of the lmax effect depends on the rest of the architecture.** OC20 S2EF-2M, 12 epochs: Lmax 6 → 20.46 meV/Å force MAE / 285 meV energy MAE; Lmax 8 → 20.46 / 279. At 20 epochs: Lmax 6 → 19.78 / 280; Lmax 8 → 19.95 / 273 (force gets *worse*, energy gets better). Mmax 2/3/4 → 20.46/285, 20.24/284, 20.24/282. The paper's statement: "increasing Lmax from 6 to 8 or Mmax from 3 to 4 **degrades the performance of eSCN on energy predictions but helps that of EquiformerV2**," and "while one might intuitively expect higher resolutions and larger models to perform better, this is only true for EquiformerV2, not eSCN." On OC22 the architecture gain is far larger than on OC20 (18.9% energy / 8.9% force vs. 4% / 9%), which the authors read as "more complicated structures can benefit more." — [EquiformerV2, arXiv 2306.12059](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.12059v3)
- **[2nd] MACE's lmax/body-order trade-off is described qualitatively in secondary sources** as "raising the message body order ν or angular bandwidth L (=ℓmax) rapidly inflates parameters and FLOPs with diminishing accuracy gains," and one downstream study reports the effect is *system-dependent* (higher ν and Lmax helped shifted bilayers but hurt twisted bilayers). **I did not read MACE's own ablation table** — treat the numbers as unverified. — [MACE, arXiv 2206.07697](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07697)

#### Virtual nodes — the claim is in the title; the ablation is absent

- **[P] VN-EGNN's Table 2 has a "VN" column that is ✓ in every VN-EGNN row.** There is no arm that removes the virtual nodes while holding features, codebase, loss and output head fixed. The only ✗ row is EGNN with numbers imported from the EquiPocket paper. Since the virtual node *is* the output mechanism in VN-EGNN ("the predicted binding site center is the position of the virtual node after L message passing layers"), removing it would require changing the readout — which is exactly why the ablation does not exist, and exactly why the claim is unsupported as an isolated architectural effect. — [VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07194)
- **[P] Placement: only one strategy is tested, and only for invariance, not accuracy.** Virtual nodes are initialised on a **Fibonacci grid** "evenly distributed across a sphere" whose radius reaches "the most distant atom," randomly rotated per sample per epoch. Table G1 reports performance across random rotations of that grid at inference: COACH420 DCC 0.612(0.005) / DCA 0.741(0.006); HOLO4K 0.524(0.002) / 0.632(0.002); PDBbind2020 0.702(0.001) / 0.833(0.002) — "there is hardly any change of the DCC and DCA metric across different random rotations." **No alternative placement (surface points, Fpocket centres, learned initial positions, centroid-only) is tested anywhere.** — [VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07194)
- **[P] Number of virtual nodes: searched, never reported.** Table E1 lists "number of virtual nodes K: {4, 8}" as a considered hyperparameter; **no per-K performance numbers appear in the paper.** — [VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07194)
- **[P] The message-passing scheme for virtual nodes IS ablated — and it is the weakest component in the paper.** The three-phase heterogeneous scheme (physical→physical, physical→virtual, virtual→physical, with "virtual node embeddings only updated once per" layer) versus homogeneous: without ESM it is null or negative on 3 of 6 metrics (see ESM section above); with ESM it gains +0.020 to +0.064. — [VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07194)
- **[P] Virtual nodes are mechanistically motivated via oversquashing, not measured.** The paper derives bounds from Topping et al. (2022, Lemma 1) showing virtual nodes "bound both the maximal path distance" — a theoretical argument. No oversquashing metric is measured with and without VNs. — [VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07194)

#### Surface representations vs. atom graphs

- **[P] EquiPocket's surface-vs-graph evidence is an *additive* ablation with 3.4× parameter growth, not an isolating one.** Table 1: EquiPocket-L (local surface geometry only) 0.15M params, COACH420 DCC 0.070(0.009) / DCA 0.171(0.008); EquiPocket-G (global atom graph, GAT+EGNN) 0.42M, 0.159(0.016) / 0.373(0.021); EquiPocket-LG 0.50M, 0.212(0.016) / 0.443(0.011); full EquiPocket (adds surface message passing + dense attention + direction loss) **1.70M**, 0.423(0.014) / 0.656(0.007). The largest single jump (LG → full, +0.211 DCC on COACH420) coincides with a 3.4× parameter increase. — [EquiPocket, arXiv 2302.12177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12177)
- **[P] Note that EquiPocket-G is statistically indistinguishable from off-the-shelf EGNN**: 0.159(0.016) vs. 0.156(0.017) on COACH420 DCC. The "global structure module" contributes essentially nothing over a plain EGNN; all of EquiPocket's gain comes from the surface machinery and the loss. — [EquiPocket, arXiv 2302.12177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12177)
- **[P] EquiPocket's better-designed *subtractive* ablation (Appendix Table 8) reports no variance at all.** EquiPocket/L (no local geometric module) COACH420 0.355/0.546, HOLO4k 0.296/0.574, PDBbind 0.465/0.606; EquiPocket/R (no global structural module) 0.364/0.541, 0.294/0.598, 0.474/0.627; EquiPocket/LR 0.308/0.502, 0.268/0.543, 0.409/0.566; full 0.423/0.656, 0.337/0.662, 0.545/0.721. Failure rate rises from 0.05 (full) to 0.09–0.16. **Single numbers, no std, no parameter counts for the arms.** — [EquiPocket, arXiv 2302.12177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12177)
- **[P] Surface generation is MSMS only; the one swept knob is probe radius, and it matters.** Table 6: radius 1 Å → 28,030 surface points, 44 GB GPU, failure 0.053, COACH420 DCC 0.433(0.018) / HOLO4K 0.338(0.008) / PDBbind 0.549(0.005); radius 1.5 Å → 24,010 points, 33 GB, 0.051, 0.423(0.014) / 0.337(0.006) / 0.545(0.010); radius 2 Å → 21,725 points, 28 GB, failure jumps to **0.096**, 0.393(0.024) / 0.289(0.004) / 0.524(0.012). Radius 1 vs 1.5 is inside the fold spread; radius 2 is clearly worse. **No MSMS vs. DMS vs. mesh comparison exists.** — [EquiPocket, arXiv 2302.12177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12177)
- **[P] DeepSurf has the single cleanest surface-side ablation in the field: surface-normal *alignment* of the local grid, one factor, same parameters.** Table 1, scPDB 5-fold CV, DCC success rate at Dcut=4 Å: ResNet-18 **without** grid alignment (33.1M params) 66.8 top-n / 69.1 top-(n+2); ResNet-18 **with** alignment (33.1M) **68.1 / 70.4** — "+1.3% in both top-n and top-(n+2) accuracies… this feature, with no additive cost, can boost the overall performance." — [DeepSurf, arXiv 2002.05643](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.05643)
- **[P] But DeepSurf's headline surface-vs-whole-protein-voxelisation claim is NOT an ablation.** It compares its own 1.0M "shallow network (DeepSite)" arm at 62.1% top-n against DeepSite's CV number, which the authors say they "derived from the provided results… is about 50%" — a figure from another paper, not measured. — [DeepSurf, arXiv 2002.05643](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.05643)

#### Attention form, gating, heads

- **[P] GDEGAN's kernel-vs-dot-product comparison is the only direct attention-form ablation in binding-site prediction, and it is favourably mis-matched in GDEGAN's favour on capacity (in the conservative direction).** Holding ESM fixed and no ADL: Gaussian dynamic attention (GDEGAN+ESM) vs. dot-product (GotenNet+ESM), COACH420 DCC 0.543(0.008) → 0.572(0.001) (+0.029), DCA 0.693(0.006) → 0.702(0.002) (+0.009); HOLO4K DCC 0.520(0.011) → 0.532(0.010) (+0.012), DCA 0.753(0.004) → 0.769(0.006) (+0.016); PDBbind DCC 0.637(0.005) → 0.652(0.010) (+0.015), DCA 0.760(0.004) → 0.810(0.011) (+0.050). The paper summarises it as "**3.33% DCC and 3.32% DCA**" relative. GDEGAN has **1.90M parameters vs. GotenNet's 2.20M** (Table 1) — the winning arm is smaller, so the effect cannot be attributed to capacity. — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] One of those six comparisons does not clear its own noise floor:** HOLO4K DCC +0.012 against reported stds of 0.011 and 0.010. The PDBbind DCA gain (+0.050 against stds 0.004/0.011) is the most robust. — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] GDEGAN's mechanistic support for GDA is a measured auxiliary quantity, which is unusually good practice:** learned variance on binding-site residues σ² = **1.41 ± 0.52** vs. non-binding **0.75 ± 0.35**, p < 0.001, over a validation set of 205,791 residues; mean head temperature evolves 2.03 → 1.826 during training with head diversity increasing 5.6×. This supports the *mechanism story*, not the accuracy attribution. — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] Number of attention heads is never ablated for accuracy.** GDEGAN's Table 5 searches heads ∈ {4, 8}; Figure 4c plots *final temperature per head*, not accuracy per head count. No head-count accuracy table exists. — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] EquiPocket ablates its "dense attention" gate (which weights surface-EGNN layers at different depths) and finds the effect is confined to small proteins.** Appendix Table 10 / Figure 5(c): "Dense Attention notably improves prediction for proteins with less than 4000 nodes. However, for larger proteins, exceeding 4000 nodes, there's no significant performance difference." No variance reported. — [EquiPocket, arXiv 2302.12177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12177)
- **[2nd] GrASP reports no component ablations at all.** An automated full-text read of the published article found 4 attention heads, ResNet skip connections, jumping-knowledge skips, Noisy Nodes regularisation and multi-aggregation (sum+mean) described but **not individually isolated**; the paper states architecture choices "were made to maximize top N DCA recall" on validation. 10-fold CV with results averaged across folds, **no standard deviations reported for main metrics**. I did not read the SI myself — flag as unconfirmed absence rather than confirmed absence. — [GrASP, PMC11182664](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11182664/)

#### Depth and width

- **[P] GDEGAN Figure 4a + Appendix Table 6 is the only published depth sweep for this task, and the exact finding is a peak at 4 layers.** Table 6, "All the other parameters were kept same to ensure fair comparison," DCC / DCA:
  | layers | params (M) | COACH420 DCC | COACH420 DCA | HOLO4K DCC | HOLO4K DCA | PDBbind DCC | PDBbind DCA |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 3 | 1.50 | 0.528(0.002) | 0.673(0.010) | 0.534(0.002) | 0.731(0.009) | 0.643(0.005) | 0.777(0.002) |
  | **4 (default)** | **1.90** | **0.580(0.008)** | **0.707(0.009)** | **0.560(0.013)** | **0.788(0.011)** | **0.675(0.010)** | **0.826(0.011)** |
  | 5 | 2.30 | 0.564(0.002) | 0.680(0.010) | 0.544(0.003) | 0.751(0.008) | 0.658(0.004) | 0.800(0.010) |
  | 6 | 2.70 | 0.550(0.005) | 0.681(0.004) | 0.541(0.002) | 0.749(0.012) | 0.660(0.010) | 0.800(0.017) |
  Text: "performance peaks at 4 layers (Average DCC=0.6050) and degrades with deeper architectures (5 layers: 0.588, 6 layers: 0.583), **despite each additional layer adding nearly 20% more parameters**." Figure 4a caption: "performance peaks at 4 layers with increased parameters leading to oversmoothing." — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] Depths tested: {3, 4, 5, 6}. Model sizes: 1.50 / 1.90 / 2.30 / 2.70 M. Hidden dimension fixed at 128 in all arms (Table 5 lists node hidden dim as the single value 128), so width is never swept.** — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] GDEGAN attributes the decline to oversmoothing by citation, not by measurement**: "Consistent with prior observations in equivariant GNNs (Zhang et al., 2024), we find that excessive message passing leads to oversmoothing—neighboring nodes become increasingly similar, losing discriminative power." No representation-similarity / Dirichlet-energy measurement is reported. — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] In the 3D-CNN family depth goes the other way, so "deeper is worse" is not a property of the task.** DeepSurf Table 1: bottleneck ResNet-18 (1.7M) 66.8 / 69.4 → bottleneck ResNet-34 (2.9M) **67.6 / 69.9**. Deeper helped. — [DeepSurf, arXiv 2002.05643](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.05643)
- **[P] VN-EGNN searched depth {2,3,4,5} and width D∈{20,30,100}, P∈{40,50,100} (Table E1) and selected 5 layers with feature/message size 100 — but reports no per-setting numbers.** So there is no second depth sweep to corroborate or contradict GDEGAN. — [VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07194)
- **[P] EquiPocket sweeps "depth of surface-egnn" in Figure 6 but the numbers are only in the figure, not in a table I could extract.** Flag as unquantified. — [EquiPocket, arXiv 2302.12177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12177)

#### Multi-task / auxiliary targets — real evidence, and it shows the benefit is conditional

- **[P] GDEGAN's auxiliary directional loss (ADL) is a clean one-factor multi-task ablation, run four ways.** Without ESM (GotenNet → GotenNet+ADL): COACH420 DCC +0.031 (0.454→0.485), HOLO4K **+0.004** (0.464(0.001)→0.468(0.004), inside spread), PDBbind +0.039 (0.553→0.592). With ESM on GotenNet: +0.013 / +0.009 / +0.012. With ESM on GDEGAN: +0.008 / +0.028 / +0.023. Paper's summary: "further improves the results by an average of 2% DCC and 3.5% DCA." — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] EquiPocket's direction loss (the same idea: predict the unit vector to the nearest ligand atom, cosine loss, L = L_b + L_d) helps small proteins and is null-to-harmful for large ones.** Appendix Table 11, DCC by protein size, without → with direction loss: 0–1000 atoms **0.319 → 0.428**; 1000–2000 0.587 → 0.621; 2000–3000 0.551 → 0.590; 3000–4000 0.371 → 0.388; **4000–5000 0.258 → 0.255 (worse)**; ≥5000 0.144 → 0.153. No variance reported. — [EquiPocket, arXiv 2302.12177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12177)
- **[2nd] Outside binding sites, auxiliary-decoder multi-task ablations are reported as non-monotone**: in drug–target affinity work "jointly optimizing both reconstruction tasks does not consistently outperform using a single decoder," and loss-weighting is decisive ("too small a coefficient may hurt… too large a coefficient may aggravate the label noise problem"). I read only search summaries. — [DecoderDTA](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41963700/); [Multi-task bioassay pre-training](https://academic.oup.com/bib/article/25/1/bbad451/7469349)

#### Recycling / iterative refinement — nothing found

- **[P] No binding-site paper I read ablates recycling or iterative refinement.** GotenNet (the GDEGAN backbone) is described as using "hierarchical tensor refinement that iteratively updates edge representations through inner product operations on high-degree steerable features," but GDEGAN inherits it as a fixed component and never ablates it. — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817); [GotenNet, ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/64d4ff4fff788cdffe236f9ce8b09400-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **[2nd] DeepPocket's two-stage design (Fpocket candidate rescoring by a 3D CNN, then 3D U-Net segmentation of the top-n pockets) is a cascade, not recycling, and I found no ablation isolating the contribution of the segmentation stage to site localisation.** — [DeepPocket](https://cvit.iiit.ac.in/images/JournalPublications/2022_01/Deeppocket.pdf)

### Inferences

- The accuracy ranking of components by *controlled* evidence in binding-site prediction is: **ESM-2 features (large, ~+0.06 to +0.15 DCC) ≫ attention form (~+0.01 to +0.03 DCC) ≈ auxiliary directional loss (~+0.01 to +0.04 DCC) ≈ depth choice (~+0.02 to +0.05 DCC between best and worst tested) ≫ heterogeneous VN message passing (0 to +0.05, conditional on ESM)**. Equivariance, lmax, virtual-node existence, virtual-node count, placement strategy, head count and width have **no controlled measurement at all**.
- Two of the headline architectural contributions in this literature are **structurally un-ablatable as designed**: VN-EGNN's virtual nodes are the output head, and GDEGAN's "SE(3)" label is a consequence of its features rather than a mechanism choice. A paper wanting to claim an equivariance effect for this task would have to build the arm itself, and the "Beyond Canonicalization" 2×2 is the template to copy.
- The one place where the geometry-removal confound can be partly separated is the comparison between NequIP's l=0 arm (geometry removed: distances only) and the "Beyond Canonicalization" scalar-message arm (geometry retained: same local frames, projected). The fact that scalarising retained geometry still costs ~0.04–0.05 cosine similarity suggests a real mechanism effect over and above the information loss — but this is on point clouds, not proteins, and the transfer is unverified.
- Because ESM dominates every other measured component by a factor of 2–5×, a binding-site architecture ablation that does not fix the feature set is close to uninterpretable; and conversely, the interaction structure in GDEGAN and VN-EGNN (ADL shrinks with ESM, heterogeneous MP grows with ESM) means **component effects measured without ESM do not transfer to the ESM regime, in either direction**.

### Gaps

- **MACE's own lmax / correlation-order ablation table.** I only have secondary descriptions; I did not read arXiv 2206.07697's ablations. Allegro and eSCN (Passaro & Zitnick 2023) lmax sweeps likewise unread — eSCN in particular is the paper that makes lmax up to 8 tractable and would be the natural place for a clean cost/accuracy-vs-lmax curve.
- **NequIP's actual lmax scan numbers.** I have the verbatim conclusion ("beyond l=1 gives a consistent improvement") and the feature-controlled setup, but not the per-l force/energy MAE values, which live in a figure I did not extract.
- **GrASP SI.** I relied on an automated read of the article body and cannot confirm the SI contains no ablations.
- **PointSite**: not reached. No ablation evidence collected.
- **EquiPocket Figure 6 numbers** (probe radius / cutoff θ / surface-EGNN depth curves) are in figure form; I extracted only the probe-radius table.
- **An independent re-run benchmark.** I found no 2025–2026 paper that retrains VN-EGNN, EquiPocket, GrASP and GDEGAN in one codebase under a shared split. UniSite (arXiv 2506.03237) explicitly does **not** re-implement prior methods — **[2nd]** "they extract results from published papers and evaluate baselines using their own code."

## Key question 2: Where ablations disagree between papers, what differs in their setup?

### Takeaway

There is exactly one direct disagreement in the binding-site literature — the magnitude and even the sign of the ESM-2 effect on DCA — and it is fully explained by what the non-ESM baseline arm contains. A second, softer disagreement about depth is explained by architecture family (GNN vs. 3D CNN).

### Cited findings

- **[P] Disagreement 1: ESM-2's effect on DCA.** GDEGAN: "+9.27% DCA averaged across datasets." VN-EGNN's homogeneous arms: COACH420 DCA +0.008 (inside spread) and **HOLO4K DCA −0.023**. — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817); [VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07194)
- **[P] Explanation for disagreement 1 — the baseline arm differs.** VN-EGNN's non-ESM arms are labelled "residue emb." and operate on Cα nodes, so the contrast is ESM-2 vs. a learned residue-identity embedding at identical graph resolution. GDEGAN's non-ESM arm uses "atomic numbers embeddings," a strictly weaker baseline, so its ESM arm plausibly also buys residue-identity information that VN-EGNN's baseline already had. — [VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07194); [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] Explanation 2 — the output head differs, and DCA is head-sensitive.** VN-EGNN regresses pocket centres directly as virtual-node coordinates, then merges with mean-shift; GDEGAN/EquiPocket classify residues/atoms and then mean-shift the positives into centres. DCA (distance from predicted centre to any ligand atom) rewards these two readouts differently, and HOLO4K's multi-chain/multi-copy structure interacts with it: VN-EGNN runs "VN-EGNN for each chain and merge[s] the predicted pocket centers," a pipeline step GDEGAN does not describe. — [VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07194); [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] Both papers flag HOLO4K as a distribution shift**, which is where most of the sign disagreement sits. GDEGAN Table 1 footnote: "holo4k contains multi chains and complex with multiple copies, presenting a strong distribution shift." VN-EGNN Table 1 footnote d: "This dataset represents a strong domain shift from the training data for all methods (except for P2Rank)." — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817); [VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07194)
- **[P] Disagreement 2: depth.** GDEGAN finds degradation beyond 4 message-passing layers (0.605 → 0.588 → 0.583 avg DCC) while DeepSurf finds a gain from 18 to 34 convolutional layers (66.8 → 67.6 top-n). Different architecture families (message-passing GNN on a residue graph vs. 3D CNN on local voxel grids); oversmoothing is a message-passing pathology that voxel CNNs with no graph aggregation do not share. — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817); [DeepSurf, arXiv 2002.05643](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.05643)
- **[P] Disagreement 3 (outside the field, but directly on the question asked): whether equivariance beats augmentation depends on the train/test rotation distribution, not on the architecture.** "Beyond Canonicalization" Table 1: augmentation 0.89 vs. best equivariant 0.88 under z/z; 0.86 vs. 0.88 under O(3)/O(3); 0.79 vs. 0.88 under z/O(3). The same paper's Figure 4 adds: the equivariant model has a steeper data-efficiency slope, "however, perhaps surprisingly, the error rate is not necessarily smaller for all dataset sizes." — [Beyond Canonicalization, arXiv 2405.15389](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15389)
- **[P-auto] Disagreement 4: whether higher lmax helps is architecture-conditional.** Same Lmax change (6→8) *degrades* eSCN's energy MAE and *improves* EquiformerV2's. — [EquiformerV2, arXiv 2306.12059](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.12059v3)

### Inferences

- Every disagreement I located is attributable to the *baseline arm* or the *evaluation regime*, never to a genuine contradiction about the mechanism. This is a strong argument that the field's ablations are under-specified rather than irreproducible.
- Because protein structures in these benchmarks are deposited in arbitrary but *fixed* orientations and all methods train with (or without) rotation augmentation on the same data, binding-site prediction sits closer to the z/z regime of "Beyond Canonicalization" (where augmentation won) than to the O(3)/O(3) regime — which would predict a *small* equivariance benefit for this task. This is an inference by analogy and has never been tested on proteins.
- HOLO4K should be reported separately in any ablation, not folded into a cross-dataset average: it is where component effects most often change sign, and both papers acknowledge it as out-of-distribution.

### Gaps

- No paper reports an ESM ablation where the non-ESM arm is explicitly a *matched-dimension learned residue embedding* at the same graph resolution and with the same output head. That is the missing decisive experiment.
- No one has tested the z/z-vs-O(3)/O(3) analogy on proteins by training the same binding-site architecture with and without rotation augmentation and with and without equivariance.

## Key question 3: Are ablations run on multiple seeds with reported seed variance?

### Takeaway

Mostly no, and where a spread is reported it is usually **fold variance, not seed variance**, and the number of runs is almost never stated. Several individually headlined effects are smaller than their own reported spread.

### Cited findings

- **[P] GDEGAN reports a spread on every ablation arm but never says how many runs produce it.** Tables 1, 2 and 6 all carry "The standard deviation is indicated in brackets"; I searched the full text for "seeds", "runs", "independent runs", "averaged over" and found no statement of the number of repetitions. Figure 4b is the one place "averaged across multiple runs" appears, and only for the temperature trace. — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] Two GDEGAN ablation effects are at or inside their noise floor.** GDA vs. dot-product on HOLO4K DCC: 0.520(0.011) → 0.532(0.010), a +0.012 effect against ~0.010 stds. ADL without ESM on HOLO4K DCC: 0.464(0.001) → 0.468(0.004), +0.004. These should not be counted as effects. — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] GDEGAN's depth effect does clear its noise floor on COACH420**: 4 layers 0.580(0.008) vs. 5 layers 0.564(0.002), a 0.016 gap against ≤0.008 stds. But 5 vs. 6 layers on PDBbind is flat: 0.658(0.004) vs. 0.660(0.010). So "monotone degradation with depth" is supported from 4→5 and not from 5→6. — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] VN-EGNN's spread is explicitly across training re-runs** ("The standard deviation across training re-runs is indicated in parentheses"), which is the right quantity — but the number of re-runs is not stated, and its spreads are large (up to 0.029 on PDBbind DCC, 0.026 on HOLO4K DCA), which is what makes its heterogeneous-MP effect unresolvable without ESM. — [VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07194)
- **[P] VN-EGNN's Table G1 spread is across random rotations of the Fibonacci grid, not across seeds**, and its means (COACH420 DCC 0.612, HOLO4K 0.524, PDBbind 0.702) differ from Table 1's re-run means (0.605, 0.532, 0.669) — so the two tables are not interchangeable and the rotation spread (0.001–0.005) must not be read as a seed spread. — [VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07194)
- **[P] EquiPocket's main-table spread is 5-fold cross-validation variance, not seeds**: "we use 5-fold cross-validation and report the mean and standard deviation." Its *better-designed* subtractive ablation (Table 8), its local-feature ablation (Table 9), its dense-attention ablation (Table 10) and its direction-loss ablation (Table 11) report **no variance whatsoever** — single numbers. — [EquiPocket, arXiv 2302.12177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12177)
- **[P] DeepSurf's Table 1 reports "average cross-validation success rates" over 5 Uniprot-split folds, with no spread.** Its two smallest effects — grid alignment (+1.3 points) and LDS vs. parameter-matched ResNet-34 (+0.7 / +0.9 points) — therefore have no error bar at all. — [DeepSurf, arXiv 2002.05643](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.05643)
- **[P] "Beyond Canonicalization" — the most carefully *designed* ablation I found — reports no variance on any accuracy table.** Tables 1–4 and 6 are single numbers; only the runtime table carries a spread (e.g. learned frames + tensor messages 0.22 ± 0.06 s per sample vs. learned frames + scalar messages 0.20 ± 0.06 s). So its headline 0.87 vs. 0.82 is a single-run comparison. — [Beyond Canonicalization, arXiv 2405.15389](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15389)
- **[P-auto] UniSite's two ablations report no variance and no significance testing.** Number of site queries 16/32/64: AP₀.₃ 0.5515 / 0.5603 / 0.5615, AP₀.₅ 0.3795 / 0.3835 / 0.3867 — differences of 0.01 and 0.007 with no error bars, which the extraction summarised as "minimal differences; no statistical significance testing." — [UniSite, arXiv 2506.03237](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03237v2)
- **[2nd] GrASP: 10-fold CV, results averaged over folds, "no explicit reporting of standard deviations or confidence intervals for main metrics."** — [GrASP, PMC11182664](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11182664/)

### Inferences

- The only two binding-site papers that report any spread on their ablation arms are GDEGAN and VN-EGNN, and neither states the number of repetitions, so the spreads cannot be converted into a standard error or a test. A reader can only do the crude check "is the effect larger than the larger of the two stds" — which GDEGAN's ESM, GDA-on-PDBbind-DCA, and depth-4-vs-5 effects pass, and GDEGAN's GDA-on-HOLO4K-DCC, GDEGAN's ADL-without-ESM-on-HOLO4K, and VN-EGNN's heterogeneous-MP-without-ESM effects fail.
- Fold variance and seed variance answer different questions (which proteins you trained on vs. which initialisation you drew) and are not substitutes. EquiPocket and DeepSurf report only the former; VN-EGNN only the latter; nobody reports both, so no paper separates data-split sensitivity from initialisation sensitivity.
- The field's effect sizes for everything except ESM features are in the 0.01–0.03 DCC range, which is the same order as the reported spreads. Any new ablation at this scale needs ≥5 seeds and a paired test to be informative.

### Gaps

- The number of repetitions behind GDEGAN's and VN-EGNN's error bars. Neither paper states it; I could not find it in the appendices I read.
- No paper in this literature reports a paired statistical test (paired bootstrap, permutation, or per-protein paired comparison) between ablation arms. All comparisons are of marginal means.

## Key question 4: Are ablation arms parameter-matched?

### Takeaway

Almost never. The binding-site literature contains exactly **two** parameter-matched or capacity-conservative arms — DeepSurf's bottleneck ResNet-34 vs. LDS-ResNet-18, and (incidentally) GDEGAN's smaller-is-better attention comparison — and the molecular-ML literature contains exactly one explicitly capacity-controlled equivariance ablation, NequIP's feature-controlled l=0 network. Everything else measures the component plus the parameters it brings.

### Cited findings

- **[P] EquiPocket's additive ablation is the worst case: 0.15M → 0.42M → 0.50M → 1.70M parameters across the four arms.** The largest claimed component effect (surface message passing, "DCC and DCA have increased by approximately 20%") coincides with a 3.4× parameter increase, so degree-of-modelling and capacity are completely entangled. Its subtractive Table 8/9 arms report no parameter counts at all. — [EquiPocket, arXiv 2302.12177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12177)
- **[P] GDEGAN's depth sweep is not parameter-matched — "each additional layer adding nearly 20% more parameters" (1.50 → 1.90 → 2.30 → 2.70 M)** — but the confound runs *against* the paper's conclusion: the worse arms are the larger ones. So the depth effect survives the capacity confound, though the sweep still cannot separate oversmoothing from optimisation difficulty or overfitting, because width is never traded against depth at fixed parameter count. — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] GDEGAN's attention-form comparison is capacity-conservative by accident: GDEGAN 1.90M vs. GotenNet 2.20M (Table 1), and GDEGAN also has lower inference time (0.032 vs. 0.049 s).** The winning arm is smaller and faster, so the +0.012 to +0.050 gains cannot be charged to parameters. This makes it the most credible non-feature component result in the field, even though it was not matched by design. — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] GDEGAN's ESM ablation is *not* parameter-matched in the input layer** — ESM-2 embeddings are high-dimensional pretrained vectors projected into the 128-dim hidden space, versus a small atomic-number embedding table — and, more importantly, it is not *information*-matched: the ESM arm imports an external pretrained model trained on UniRef, so the comparison measures "transfer from a protein LM" rather than "a feature channel." — [GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19817)
- **[P] VN-EGNN reports a single parameter count (1.20M) for VN-EGNN in Table 1 and gives no parameter counts for any of the four ablation arms in Table 2.** Dropping heterogeneous message passing removes two of three message-passing phases and therefore removes parameters, so the heterogeneous-MP effect is degree-of-coupling plus capacity. — [VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07194)
- **[P] DeepSurf is the one binding-site paper that builds a parameter-matched arm on purpose and says so.** "For a fairer assessment of the contribution of the LDS block, we tested DeepSurf employed with a bottleneck ResNet with 34 layers, which has the same parameters as the bottleneck LDS-ResNet-18." Result: bottleneck ResNet-34 (2.9M) 67.6 / 69.9 vs. bottleneck LDS-ResNet-18 (3.0M) 68.3 / 70.8 — "the LDS variant achieved 0.7% higher top-n accuracy and 0.9% higher top-(n+2) accuracy." Its grid-alignment ablation is also exactly matched (both arms 33.1M). — [DeepSurf, arXiv 2002.05643](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.05643)
- **[P] NequIP is the one capacity-controlled equivariance/degree ablation in molecular ML.** "To control for the different number of weights and features in orders of different rotation order l, we report weight- and feature-controlled data in Appendix B. Both show qualitatively the same effect." The construction: "a l = 0 network with the same number of features as the l = 1 network, i.e. 4x more features than the original l = 0 network (1 scalar and 3 vector features), in particular the l = 1 network had a feature configuration of 64x0o + 64x0e + 64x1o + 64x1e while the original l = 0 network used 64x0e and feature-controlled l = 0 network used 512x0e." — [NequIP, arXiv 2101.03164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03164)
- **[P] "Beyond Canonicalization" matches hyperparameters but not parameters.** It states the comparisons were made "always for a fair comparison using the same hyperparameters in architecture and optimizer," but also notes random frames have "fewer learnable parameters since the local frames are not learned," and tensor messages necessarily add channels over scalar messages. So its 2×2 is hyperparameter-matched, not parameter-matched; its runtime table quantifies the residual cost (scalar 0.20 s vs. tensor 0.22 s per sample with learned frames). — [Beyond Canonicalization, arXiv 2405.15389](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15389)
- **[P-auto] EquiformerV2's Lmax sweep gives no parameter counts in the rows I extracted**, so Lmax 6 → 8 is degree plus capacity; the authors' own framing (that the same Lmax increase helps EquiformerV2 and hurts eSCN) implies the effect is not capacity-driven, since both would gain parameters. — [EquiformerV2, arXiv 2306.12059](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.12059v3)

### Inferences

- The brief's concern is exactly right and under-appreciated: in this literature, "removing tensor channels also removes parameters" is the normal case, and no binding-site paper has ever removed tensor channels at all, so the question has never even been posed for this task.
- NequIP's feature-controlled result is the single most load-bearing piece of evidence that degree ≥1 is not a capacity proxy — but because its l=0 arm is SchNet-style (distances only), it conflates "no equivariant mechanism" with "no angular input." The honest reading is: **capacity-matched, geometry-unmatched**. "Beyond Canonicalization" is the mirror image: **geometry-matched, capacity-unmatched**. Together they bracket the effect; neither alone establishes it.
- A defensible EquiCave-style ablation plan would therefore need, at minimum: (a) an arm that scalarises the same local-frame geometry with the output head held equivariant and the parameter count matched by widening the scalar channels (the NequIP 512x0e trick applied in the "Beyond Canonicalization" design); (b) an lmax ∈ {1,2,3} sweep with hidden width tuned to hold parameters fixed; (c) ≥5 seeds with paired per-protein tests; (d) HOLO4K reported separately.

### Gaps

- No paper reports parameter counts for every arm of an ablation table alongside a spread. GDEGAN gives params in Table 1 and Table 6 but not in Table 2; VN-EGNN and EquiPocket give them only for full models or additive variants.
- I found no binding-site paper that holds parameters fixed while trading depth against width, so GDEGAN's "oversmoothing" attribution remains untested against the simpler explanations.
- eSCN (Passaro & Zitnick 2023) and Allegro were not read; their lmax cost/accuracy curves, which would be the best source for "where higher degrees stop paying and why," remain unverified.
