# Chemistry Pays Three Ways In Pocket Ranking

The honest answer to "how should chemical information be represented inside a deep binding-site model" is: **thinly, cheaply, learned from atom types and distances, and on node scalars — because every expensive physical channel that has been tested head-to-head has lost.** The single strongest controlled result in this literature is the MaSIF → dMaSIF pair: MaSIF fed an APBS Poisson–Boltzmann potential per surface vertex and reached 0.85 site ROC-AUC; dMaSIF deleted the PB solve entirely, replaced it with a small MLP over `[atom type, 1/r]` for the 16 nearest atoms, **matched 0.85 and then beat it at 0.87**, while cutting preprocessing from **76.45 s to 66 ms per protein** ([dMaSIF, CVPR 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/Sverrisson_Fast_End-to-End_Learning_on_Protein_Surfaces_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf)). The mechanism is published too — the learned feature regresses the PB potential at **r = 0.83** — so the physics channel was largely re-deriving information already present in the input. Against that, only three chemistry-adjacent representations have real measured gains on site tasks: **evolutionary information** (ESM-2 node scalars, +0.06 to +0.12 DCC in two independent ablations; MSA conservation, +2.0 Top-(N+2) points), **atom-type occupancy** (SwinSite's largest masking drop is `atom:O` at 39.7%, not a charge channel), and **metal presence** (removing ion sites from LIGYSIS raises every method's recall by 5–10 points). Everything else in the brief's priority list — partial charges, continuum electrostatics, hydration thermodynamics, protonation states, tautomers, multipoles, learned charges — has **either no ablation at all on a small-molecule pocket benchmark, or a controlled refutation**. For a model that already carries per-probe pharmacophore availability, the asking project's own three nulls (frozen ESM-2 650M −0.010, SAS surface module −0.011, degree-2 tensors −0.006, seed sd 0.009) are the result this literature predicts, and they predict further nulls for most of what remains.

**Provenance convention used throughout.** The notes distinguish facts read from a primary source in-session (marked here **[verified]**) from facts taken from a search engine's synthesis of a source that was not opened (**[second-hand]**), and separately distinguish a feature a paper merely *names* as an input (**[listed]**) from one a paper ran an experiment to isolate (**[measured]**). Those markings are preserved below and are never upgraded. Software licences are the weakest part of the whole evidence base and are reported as the notes found them, including where a paper publishes a repository URL and names no licence at all.

## dMaSIF deleted the Poisson–Boltzmann solve, got faster, and got better

Electrostatics is the priority-one topic and it has the cleanest negative answer. The field runs two featurization monocultures. One descends from Pafnucy's 19-feature atom descriptor, which contains exactly **one float partial-charge channel** produced by UCSF Chimera with "Amber ff14SB for standard residues and AM1-BCC for non-standard residues and ligands" ([Pafnucy, arXiv:1712.07042](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.07042)) **[verified]**. That descriptor propagated verbatim into Kalasanty ("Proteins were described with 18 atomic features used in our previous project") ([Kalasanty, arXiv:1904.06517](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.06517)) and DeepSurf ([DeepSurf, arXiv:2002.05643](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.05643)) **[verified, listed]**, and in modified form into SwinSite's "estimated partial charge" ([SwinSite, PMC12977039](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12977039/)) **[verified, listed]**. The other family — DeepSite/KDEEP via moleculekit's eight default channels including `positive_ionizable`/`negative_ionizable` ([moleculekit docs](https://software.acellera.com/moleculekit/moleculekit.tools.voxeldescriptors.html)), DeepPocket's 14 atom-type channels, gnina's 34–35 smina types — carries **no partial charge and no electrostatic potential at all**, and Vina-style scoring has no electrostatic term whatever ([gnina workshop notes](https://gnina.github.io/gnina/rsc_workshop2021/)) **[second-hand]**. Both families reach broadly comparable site numbers, so the charge channel is not what separates good predictors from bad ones.

Critically, **none of Pafnucy, Kalasanty or DeepSurf ever measured the charge channel**. Pafnucy's only quantitative signal is Figure 3's first-layer weight ranges, where `partialcharge` ranks 4th of 17 — an inspection of weight magnitudes, not a retrained ablation, and plausibly an artefact of the charges being std-normalised while most other channels are 0/1 bits **[verified, explicitly not an ablation]**. Kalasanty ran no input-feature ablation of any kind; DeepSurf's ablations are entirely architectural (ResNet vs LDS-ResNet, depth, surface alignment) **[verified, not measured]**. SwinSite is the only site model with a per-channel ablation, and it masks each channel to zero at inference with weights fixed — a biased estimator that bounds importance from above, as the pathological result that masking `hybridization` drives success to exactly zero demonstrates. Its main-text numbers are Top-1 DCC success drops of **39.7% (`atom:O`), 25.45% (`atom:C`), 14.88% (`atom:N`)**, with H-bond donor, aromatic, acceptor and hydrophobic around 15–23% each; the authors conclude chemical features matter "to a lesser extent than geometric cues" ([SwinSite, PMC12977039](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12977039/)) **[verified]**. **The partial-charge number sits in Supporting Information that the notes could not retrieve — it is the single most directly relevant missing figure in the whole brief.** Two indexed versions of the SwinSite paper also report different Kalasanty baselines on the same benchmarks (36.29/26.96/38.01/31.75/32.35 versus 56.45/53.91/61.99/52.33/54.39), so its comparison table must be version-checked before use **[second-hand, flagged as a discrepancy]**.

Continuum electrostatics has exactly one reference implementation. MaSIF feeds an APBS 1.5 Poisson–Boltzmann potential, prepared with PDB2PQR, interpolated to mesh vertices with Multivalue, capped at ±30 and normalised to [−1, 1], as one of five channels, and it ran a genuine **retrained** feature-subset ablation (Geom / hbond / elec / hpathy / G+C over 218,246 positive and 1,973,624 negative surface points) ([MaSIF, Nat Methods 2020](https://www-cbi.cs.uni-saarland.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/MaSIF-paper_SS2025.pdf)) **[verified, measured]**. The recoverable bar values are **0.68, 0.75, 0.75, 0.78, 0.80 ROC AUC**, with all-features highest at 0.80 — but the notes could not unambiguously assign each value to its bar from the PDF text layer, so **the ROC AUC of the electrostatics-only MaSIF-site model is not established**, and a search engine's claim that electrostatics "appears to be the strongest single feature" is explicitly **not endorsed**. What is established is the shape: every single-feature subset lands within 0.02–0.12 of the full model, on a protein–protein interface task rather than small-molecule pockets.

The cost figures are the other half of the argument, and they are published. From dMaSIF's Table 1, MaSIF's per-protein preprocessing is **6.11 ± 6.18 s surface generation + 19.69 ± 16.08 s input features (the step containing the APBS solve) + 50.65 ± 45.15 s local coordinates = 76.45 s**, with cached features exceeding **1 TB**; dMaSIF's equivalents are **59.0 ms + 6.59 ms + 0.46 ms = 66 ms** in batches of 128, which the authors describe as "about 1000 times faster" **[verified]**. The note author's own arithmetic on those published numbers puts the PB-bearing feature step at **≈120×** MaSIF's 164 ms forward pass and **≈550×** dMaSIF's 36 ms three-layer pass, and MaSIF's total precomputation at **≈466×** its own inference — **inference from verified numbers, not quoted figures**. Two further details matter for anyone budgeting: the PB solve is *not* MaSIF's dominant cost (geodesic local coordinates at 50.65 s are 2.6× more expensive than the whole feature step), and APBS scaling is unfavourable, with "the memory cost grows linearly and the time to solution grows quadratically with respect to the grid size" ([Bempp-Exafmm, arXiv:2103.01048](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.01048)) **[second-hand]**. **No published APBS wall-clock exists for a ~300-residue protein at 0.5 Å** — the notes searched for it specifically and found none.

Three adjacent findings close the electrostatics question. First, the plain-Coulomb substitute nobody has tested: **no paper compares a bare or screened (Debye–Hückel) Coulomb grid against a PB grid as a network input, for site detection, affinity or pose scoring**. That is a clean, cheap, unpublished experiment. Second, learned charges: the QEq/EEM/4G-HDNNP literature is mature and well-benchmarked, but every controlled comparison in it is scored on energies, forces, dipoles or Hirshfeld charges, on NaCl clusters, carbon chains, silver clusters, slabs or bulk water ([4G-HDNNP, Nat Commun 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-20427-2); [Variational charge equilibration, npj Comput Mater](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41524-024-01225-6)) **[second-hand]**. **No evidence exists that learned or equilibrated charges beat fixed force-field charges on any downstream structural task**, and the physical condition where QEq demonstrably wins — global, non-local charge redistribution in a doped slab — has no analogue in site detection, where the question is local and the protein's global charge state is fixed. Third, multipoles appear in equivariant networks almost exclusively as *outputs*; the one ablation-shaped result anywhere reports that adding atomic dipoles on top of charges plus quadrupoles performed **worse** and overfit more ([ML dipole/quadrupole ESP study](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/3050-287X/ae531a)) **[second-hand, measured, on ESP reproduction not a structural task]**. An `l=1` internal channel is not a dipole moment, and the PaiNN-style evidence that vector features help is a different claim from "physically-derived multipole inputs help."

Finally, the one direct measurement of charge features for *ranking* is damning. P2Rank's 35 features contain no electrostatics, "the single most important feature turned out to be a geometric feature termed protrusion" ([P2Rank, J Cheminform](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13321-018-0285-8)) **[second-hand]**, and in its own importance table the charge-related features sit at the very bottom — **vsCation 0.00083, amide 0.00083, posCharge 0.0010, hBondDonor 0.0011**, against protrusion at **0.0845** and B-factor at **0.0139** ([P2Rank SI](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1186%2Fs13321-018-0285-8/MediaObjects/13321_2018_285_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)) **[verified]**. The measured hierarchy is buriedness ≫ local flexibility > aromatic/hydrophobic/acceptor character > conservation ≫ charge. One caveat runs the other way and deserves a stratified test rather than dismissal: DeepDrug3D's shape-only ablation, which replaced 14 DFIRE knowledge-based energy channels with a 2-channel binary occupancy, lost **~0.13 on nucleotide pockets and ~0 on heme pockets** against a ~0.95 full-model accuracy ([DeepDrug3D, PLoS Comput Biol](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1006718)) **[second-hand, measured]**. Aggregate DCC/DCA on COACH420 would average exactly that heterogeneity away, and **no paper stratifies a site-prediction ablation by pocket electrostatic character**.

Code and licence status: Pafnucy ([gitlab.com/cheminfIBB/pafnucy](https://gitlab.com/cheminfIBB/pafnucy/-/tree/master)), Kalasanty (source **and weights**), DeepSurf ([github.com/stemylonas/DeepSurf](https://github.com/stemylonas/DeepSurf), source and **trained models**), SwinSite (github.com/ding-oh/SwinSite, weights not mentioned), GrASP ([github.com/tiwarylab/GrASP](https://github.com/tiwarylab/GrASP), model and data) and VN-EGNN all publish repositories and **none of the papers states a software licence**; the notes did not verify licence files in the repos, so **treat all licence questions here as unresolved**. MaSIF's chain pins PDB2PQR 2.1.1, APBS 1.5, MSMS and Reduce ([masif repo](https://github.com/LPDI-EPFL/masif)) **[second-hand]**; dMaSIF's licence was not verified. EquiPocket (arXiv:2302.12177) is **withdrawn and serves no PDF**, so it is uncitable for any feature claim ([arXiv abstract page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12177)) **[verified]**.

## Desolvation has exactly one benchmarked precedent, from 2012, and no successor

The hydration question splits cleanly. The strict claim — that no explicit-water hydration-thermodynamics descriptor (WaterMap/GIST/3D-RISM/SZMAP/JAWS class) has ever been turned into a cavity-ranking feature and benchmarked on COACH420, HOLO4K, LIGYSIS or sc-PDB — **holds**. The loose claim fails on one counterexample: **dPredGB** (Schneider & Zacharias 2012) detects cavities geometrically and then ranks the full candidate list by a generalized-Born desolvation free energy, reporting the known cavity as top-ranked in **69% of unbound and 85% of bound cases** ([OSTI 1059618](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1059618)) **[primary abstract only; full text not readable — ScienceDirect 403]**. That is exactly the architecture the brief asks about, implemented fourteen years ago, and essentially never followed up. The note author's sharpest observation is a deflating one: **Fpocket reports 83%/92% top-1/top-3 on bound and 69%/94% on unbound** ([Fpocket, BMC Bioinformatics 10:168](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2105-10-168)) **[second-hand]** — numbers nearly indistinguishable from dPredGB's on a similarly vintage benchmark, which is the strongest available reason to doubt that the desolvation term added anything over geometry. Resolving it requires the dPredGB full text, which remains the highest-value single follow-up in the hydration notes.

Everything in the WaterMap/GIST/3D-RISM family is disqualified for a per-protein input channel by cost, and the licence picture is decisive. **WaterMap is commercial (Schrödinger), SZMAP commercial (OpenEye), WaterFLAP commercial (Cresset/Molecular Discovery); GIST and 3D-RISM ship free inside AmberTools; JAWS is entirely uncovered** in the notes. No per-run wall-clock figure for WaterMap was found from any source. The one properly verified 3D-RISM number is "the computation for each ligand-free structure took less than 2 hr" on the HOKUSAI supercomputer, from the 3,706-structure survey ([PMC7540010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7540010/)) **[verified]**; other reported figures span ~30 minutes to tens of hours and are mutually inconsistent across hardware **[second-hand]**. Only a HydraMap-class statistical potential approaches a usable budget, reportedly matching 3D-RISM and WATsite on external tests at **30–1,000× the speed** ([HydraMap v2, PubMed 37433022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37433022/)) **[second-hand]**, and its licence and runnability are **unverified**. The nearest existing instance of water thermodynamics as a network channel is DeepWATsite's CNN, which puts occupancy, enthalpy and entropy on a 3D grid alongside protein and ligand density — but trained to separate active from decoy *poses* in a known site ([Commun Chem 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42004-020-0261-x)) **[second-hand]**.

The "unhappy water" hypothesis is well-evidenced for *individual waters inside a known pocket* and completely unmeasured for *whole cavities*. ColdBrew is the large-N anchor: 242 matched cryo/room-temperature structure pairs, **71,145 classified cryo waters** of which **32,901 are present at RT and 38,244 are not** — i.e. **54% of ordered cryo waters are freezing artefacts**, in a PDB that is **95% cryo** ([PMC12822489](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12822489); [PMC12585415](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12585415/)) **[verified]**. Its random forest over five cheap metrics reaches **AUC 0.810 train / 0.799 test**, rising to **0.838** within 7 Å of a ligand, with single-metric AUCs of **EDIA 0.747, Bnorm 0.734, RSCC 0.725, SASA 0.720, H-bond count 0.672**, and it correlates with 20 ns MD+GIST at **r = −0.60** overall and **−0.62** near the ligand **[verified]**. Two things follow. First, three of those five metrics (Bnorm, RSCC, EDIA) are crystallographic refinement quantities, so a model reading deposited waters learns resolution, temperature and depositor practice — **stripping waters is methodologically correct**, and the brief's question is slightly mis-posed. Second, and this is the quantitative case for a cheap channel, **the two metrics computable from coordinates alone (SASA 0.720, H-bond count 0.672) are not far behind the full five-metric 0.804**: most of the water-stability signal is geometric and chemical, not experimental.

The hypothesis's direction is also subtler than it looks. ColdBrew finds waters displaced by **polar** ligand atoms had median stability probability **0.71** versus **0.38** for those displaced by **nonpolar** atoms — ligands displace both stable and unstable waters, and which one tells you about the chemistry of the displacing group, not about whether the site is bindable **[verified]**. A cavity-level "mean water unhappiness" feature would conflate two signals; the defensible formulation is compositional (a *population* of low-stability waters that hydrophobic ligand atoms can take over cheaply) — and that is close to, possibly redundant with, SiteMap's hydrophobic-enclosure term. **Dscore already is the cheap desolvation feature**: a weighted sum of enclosure, size and a *negative hydrophilicity* term, with Schrödinger's own note that "undruggable and difficult sites typically are much more hydrophilic and much less hydrophobic than druggable sites" ([Schrödinger support 320](https://my.schrodinger.com/support/article/320)) **[second-hand]**. It is commercial, no wall-clock is published, and its thresholds disagree sharply across the literature — druggable >1.108 in Halgren's commonly cited values, >0.98 in a patent, >0.8 in a three-tier scheme — so **cross-study Dscore comparisons are unsafe** and the number cannot be imported as a label or comparator **[second-hand]**. The sharp consequence for any new hydration channel: it must be ablated against an enclosure-weighted hydrophobicity baseline, not against raw buriedness, or it is a reinvention of Dscore.

Explicit water-position prediction is now mature — SuperWater (score-based diffusion plus an SE(3)-equivariant confidence model, **MAD 0.3 ± 0.06 Å** for true positives at cap 0.5, code at [github.com/kuangxh9/SuperWater](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.11.18.624208v1.full), **licence not stated, weights not explicitly mentioned, no inference wall-clock reported**) **[verified]**, WaterFlow ([github.com/prism-science/WaterFlow](https://github.com/prism-science/WaterFlow), clearest open-weights candidate, licence unconfirmed) **[second-hand]**, HydraProt, GalaxyWater-CNN and WatGNN. **None predicts water thermodynamics, none reports per-protein inference time, and none has been applied to pocket detection** — SuperWater lists "binding site predictions" as future work. Two models output a continuous field rather than discrete positions and are therefore drop-in channel candidates: the 2020 hydration-occupancy CNN ([Commun Chem s42004-020-00435-5](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42004-020-00435-5)) and gr Predictor ([bioRxiv 2022.04.18.488616](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.04.18.488616.full.pdf)) **[second-hand]**. Using any of them creates a leakage problem, since all were trained on PDB crystal waters that overlap COACH420/HOLO4K/sc-PDB heavily. And the apo penalty is quantified by analogy: MD-derived hydration sites reproduced **73%** of crystal binding-site waters with the ligand present and **58%** without it ([JCIM 2017](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jcim.7b00520)) **[second-hand]**, while in Hsp90α one water was conserved in **88%** of loop-in structures but displaced in **18%** of helical ones **[verified]** — any water-derived channel will be substantially weaker exactly where cavity ranking is hardest.

The crucial missing measurement is a base rate: **no source gives the fraction of true binding cavities containing ≥1 ordered water versus the fraction of decoy cavities that do**. Without those two numbers the "are buried waters predictive of bindability" question cannot be answered, and the notes judge it computable in-house in an afternoon.

## Protonation and metals are stripped silently, and never ablated

Protonation is the clearest void in the brief. **No structure-based binding-site predictor conditions on a predicted protonation state or pKa.** Protonation enters at most indirectly and silently: through Open Babel partial charges and SMARTS donor/acceptor bits computed on heavy atoms (Kalasanty, DeepSurf), through a `formal_charge` node feature (GrASP), or not at all (VN-EGNN, which is Cα-level and so cannot represent it) **[verified]**. DeepSurf explicitly removes hydrogens before site extraction; DeepPocket removes "all heteroatoms and solvent molecules" with Biopython; GrASP's repository states heteroatoms "will be cleaned during parsing"; P2Rank's full text, grepped end to end, contains **no mention of hydrogens, protonation, pKa or HETATM atom-level handling** — only a HET-group filter for defining ligands **[verified]**. Adding hydrogens is not standard practice; where papers mention hydrogens at all, it is to delete them. The one place it is done properly is upstream curation: sc-PDB adds hydrogens, charges Arg/Lys positive and Asp/Glu negative, protonates other residues from ionized templates and optimizes the H-bond network with Hydescorer — **no pH stated** ([sc-PDB, PMC4384012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4384012/)) **[verified]**. The note author's sharp observation: **the protonation information survives only in the labels, not the inputs**, because VolSite derives pharmacophoric cavity labels from the protonated structure and then every downstream consumer throws the hydrogens away. A model cannot in principle tell HID from HIE from HIP, or neutral Cys from thiolate.

Does it matter? **There is no measurement at all of protonation changing which pocket a predictor picks or how it ranks pockets.** Every piece of evidence is downstream. Virtual screening on *M. tuberculosis* RmlC is measurably sensitive: varying two histidines' protonation and rotamer states gave receptor models with "AUC values range from 0.868 to 0.996" **[primary snippet]**. Which site gets occupied can flip in MD: for trypsin, benzamidine binds the S1 pocket in "48% of HID57 simulations, 44% of HIE57, and 10% of HIP57" replicas, driven by a histidine more than 10 Å away ([PMC9289141](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9289141/)) **[primary snippet]**. Tools disagree exactly where it matters — PROPKA 3, H++ and DelPhiPKa gave **6.16, 5.75 and 6.77** for the same histidine, all straddling pH 7 ([Biophys J](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0006349523041516)) **[primary snippet]** — and **no published statistic exists for what fraction of His residues in a benchmark are ambiguous**. The note author's inference, labelled as such: pocket *detection* is probably robust because the DCA/DCC criteria are coarse at 4 Å and a reversed H-bond vector will not move a pocket centre that far; if the effect exists it will show up in *ranking*, and running a predictor on HID/HIE/HIP variants to measure rank churn is a cheap experiment nobody has published.

If a protonation feature is wanted, the cost and licence picture is well characterised. **PROPKA 3** is LGPL-2.1 per the official docs and PyPI, though one older conda listing says LGPL-3.0 — **conflict, check the repo LICENSE**; throughput ~**38 pKa/s** **[primary snippet]**. **PypKa** is LGPL-3.0 for the Python module but its DelPhi solver dependency is **proprietary**, a real redistribution blocker; its wall-clock table is the only properly tabulated one for any PB method, from **12 s for 1A1W (83 residues, 23 titratable) to 24 min 06 s for 1CB5 (1359 residues, 405 titratable)** on 16 cores, against **~1–4 s for pKAI** and **~0.5 s for a pKPDB lookup** ([PypKa server, NAR 2024](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/52/W1/W294/7645774)) **[primary snippet]**. **H++** is a web service with no licence text found: ~18 s for 12 titratable sites, 5 min for 111, 45 min for 360 **[primary snippet]**. The ML generation is far cheaper — **pKALM** is sequence-only, RMSE 0.8658, throughput **~4,965 pKa/s**, with the preprint text under CC-BY-NC-ND (a document licence, not a code licence) **[primary snippet]**; **pKAI**'s and **DeepKa**'s code licences were **not verified**. The honest accuracy picture comes from the independent benchmark: on 408 curated PKAD residues, DeepKa was best (MUE 0.60, RMSE 0.81) but **"no method clearly outperformed the null hypotheses with confidence"** ([JCIM 2023 / PMC10466379](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10466379/)) **[primary snippet]**. Since the accuracy spread between tools (RMSE 0.81–1.11) is smaller than the ~1 unit needed to call a His protonated at pH 7, **tool choice matters less than the fact that no single tool's answer is decisive** — a pKa prediction belongs in a model as a soft feature, not a hard assignment.

Metals are the one place in this section with a price tag. Three regimes exist: DeepSite has a dedicated `metallic` channel among its eight (though "non-protein atoms are filtered out of the calculation," which leaves it **ambiguous** whether an isolated Zn HETATM reaches the grid) **[primary snippet]**; Kalasanty and DeepSurf carry a `metal` bit inside their 9-way atom-type one-hot, so metals are represented *if present in the input file* **[verified]**; DeepPocket and GrASP strip heteroatoms, so their metal representation is always zero **[verified]**; VN-EGNN cannot represent a metal at all **[verified]**. P2Rank's handling is **not stated**. The note author identifies an unremarked train/test inconsistency: Kalasanty and DeepSurf train on sc-PDB protein files that "may include cofactor(s), metallic ion(s) and covalently bound residue(s)" but are evaluated on PDB files whose heteroatom handling is different or unstated. **No metal-channel ablation exists for DeepSite, Kalasanty, DeepSurf, DeepPocket, P2Rank, EquiPocket, VN-EGNN or GrASP.** The only quantitative evidence is indirect and is the strongest single number in these notes for "getting it wrong costs something": removing ions from LIGYSIS **raises top-(N+2) recall by 5–10 percentage points for all methods except fpocket**, without changing the method ranking ([J Cheminform 2024 / PMC11552181](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11552181/)) **[verified]**. It conflates two causes — metals missing from the input, and ion sites being small and geometrically atypical (56% of LIGYSIS sites are 1–10 residues with ions, 36% without) — so it cannot be charged to representation alone.

Prevalence matters for interpreting any of this. **Ions are ≈40% of LIGYSIS's ligand sites**, with Zn²⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺ and Mn²⁺ four of its five most frequent ligands, together 19.2% of all ligands **[verified]**. In COACH420 and HOLO4K the figure is near zero **by construction**, because P2Rank's relevant-ligand filter requires "number of ligand atoms is greater or equal than 5," excluding every monatomic ion, and the authors state the design choice plainly: "We believe that predicting binding sites for ions, peptides and other specific types of binding partners would be better served by specialized methods" ([P2Rank SI](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1186%2Fs13321-018-0285-8/MediaObjects/13321_2018_285_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)) **[verified]**. Cofactors run the opposite way: **HEM, FAD, NAD, NAP, ATP and ADP are not on P2Rank's ignore list** (HOH, DOD, WAT, NAG, MAN, UNK, GLC, ABA, MPD, GOL, SO4, PO4) and all have ≥5 atoms, so they are relevant ligands and define real targets — and the LIGYSIS authors characterise every dataset except LIGYSIS, LIGYSIS_NI and PDBbind_REF as "dominated by co-factor ligands" **[verified]**. The resulting distortion is that COACH420/HOLO4K over-represent large, buried, highly conserved cofactor pockets and under-represent small ion sites and shallow drug-like pockets; a method tuned on them is tuned for big buried cavities. Worse, **the same chemical entity changes role between benchmark families**: sc-PDB puts cofactors on the protein side, COACH420/HOLO4K put them on the ligand side, so training on sc-PDB and testing on HOLO4K inverts the status of a given HEM. The notes found no paper that notes this.

For a metal input channel, **AllMetal3D is the sensible choice**: multi-metal (11 ions), `pip install allmetal3d`, ChimeraX extension, Zenodo-archived, and **unambiguously MIT-licensed** ([bioRxiv 2025.02.05.636627](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.05.636627v1); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/allmetal3d/)) **[primary snippet]**. Metal3D is the better-documented accuracy reference — predictions within **0.70 ± 0.64 Å** of experimental positions, **25 s for a 250-residue protein** on 20 CPUs and a GTX2070 — but it is zinc-only, **does not state a code licence**, and does not say whether weights are released separately ([Metal3D, PMC10175565](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10175565/)) **[verified]**. BioMetAll carries a **licence conflict** (page text and PyPI say BSD-3-Clause, the GitHub sidebar shows LGPL-3.0); MIB2's code, weights and licence were **not found**; AlphaFill, the right tool for transplanting metals and cofactors into apo or predicted structures, is **BSD-2-Clause**, though the AlphaFill databank's own data licence is **not stated** ([AlphaFill, PMC9911346](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9911346/)) **[primary snippet]**. One design caution with teeth: Metal3D's authors estimate **about one third of zinc sites in the PDB are artifacts**, and BioLiP decides a metal's status by reading the primary citation's abstract case by case **[verified]** — so a metal channel should carry a confidence (occupancy, coordination number, or an AllMetal3D probability) rather than a hard presence bit, or it learns crystallization noise.

## Evolutionary information is the only chemistry-adjacent channel with a large measured effect — and it reverses under strict splits

Conservation and coevolution are where the measured effects actually live, and the two routes behave very differently. The **learned/transferred** route is ESM-2 node scalars, and it has the strongest controlled evidence in the entire binding-site literature. GDEGAN's Table 2 is a clean one-factor swap: replacing atomic-number embeddings with ESM-2, architecture fixed, moves COACH420 DCC **0.454(0.007) → 0.543(0.008)**, HOLO4K **0.464(0.001) → 0.520(0.011)**, PDBbind2020 **0.553(0.008) → 0.637(0.005)** — summarised by the authors as "**15.61% DCC and 9.27% DCA averaged across datasets**" ([GDEGAN, arXiv 2603.19817](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.19817)) **[verified, measured]**. VN-EGNN runs an independent and tighter version, one-hot amino acid versus ESM-2 at identical Cα resolution: COACH420 DCC **0.503(0.022) → 0.605(0.009)**, HOLO4K **0.438(0.019) → 0.532(0.021)**, PDBbind **0.551(0.017) → 0.669(0.015)** ([VN-EGNN, arXiv 2404.07194](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2404.07194)) **[verified, measured]**. By controlled evidence the component ranking for this task is **ESM-2 features (+0.06 to +0.15 DCC) ≫ attention form (+0.01 to +0.03) ≈ auxiliary directional loss (+0.01 to +0.04) ≈ depth choice (+0.02 to +0.05) ≫ heterogeneous virtual-node message passing (0 to +0.05, conditional on ESM)**; equivariance, `l_max`, virtual-node existence, virtual-node count and placement, head count and width have **no controlled measurement at all** in this field.

But the ESM effect is **not uniform and not split-invariant**, and this is the single most important mechanistic finding for the asking project. Inside VN-EGNN's own table, HOLO4K DCA goes the *wrong* way with ESM on (0.618(0.024) → 0.595(0.010), **−0.023**) and COACH420 DCA moves only +0.008, inside the spread **[verified]**. GDEGAN's non-ESM arm is atomic numbers — a strictly weaker baseline than VN-EGNN's learned residue embedding — which explains the magnitude disagreement without any contradiction about mechanism. The decisive evidence is HonestAffinity, the best-designed ablation in the set (three seeds for every variant and every reproduced baseline, identical schedule and data, only the protein token representation swapped, paired bootstrap checks on selected comparisons): ESM-2 versus a learned 21-vocabulary residue embedding gives ΔR favouring ESM on familiar splits (**val +0.019, CASF-2016 +0.034**) and favouring the *learned* embedding on strict no-leak tiers (**−0.024, −0.042, −0.064** on test_cl1/cl2/cl3) ([HonestAffinity, arXiv 2606.03422](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03422)) **[verified]**. Their mechanism: PLM features encode "within-family discriminators, such as residue conservation, secondary-structure cues, and binding-site signatures, that are most informative when the test target's protein family overlaps the training distribution." Their framing is the most quotable sentence in the whole brief — two unrelated architectural choices "both change sign between canonical and leak-proof evaluations in the same direction … **Single-regime reporting would have concealed both reversals.**" AtomSurf reaches the same conclusion independently: removing ESM lowers performance, but the model "stays competitive," and **the drop is smaller on PINDER's stricter splits**, with the authors hypothesising that "ESM-derived features may aid memorization, while structural features may generalize better" ([AtomSurf, arXiv 2309.16519v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.16519v4)) **[verified, flagged by its authors as a hypothesis]**. **The asking project's −0.010 for frozen ESM-2 650M is the expected value under this literature, not an anomaly.**

The **computed-from-the-target's-own-MSA** route behaves differently and is the more interesting lever for ranking. P2Rank's 2018 paper did *not* use conservation — it is listed as future work and absent from the 35-feature table — but the shipped `conservation`-enabled model, **P2Rank_CONS**, reaches **Top-(N+2) recall 53.9% versus 51.9%** for default P2Rank on LIGYSIS, with **+346 true positives** at the fixed 100-false-positive operating point ([Utgés & Barton 2024, PMC11552181](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11552181/)) **[primary-summarised]**. LIGYSIS was built with 0.5–9.7% overlap with sc-PDB, Binding MOAD and CHEN11, so **that gain is measured on largely train-dissimilar structures** — and because conservation is computed per-target from an MSA at inference, **its contribution carries no training-set dependence at all**. That is a structurally different property from every learned channel, and it is precisely the property the ESM reversal lacks. The counter-consideration is real: PRANK deliberately *excluded* conservation, using "only local geometric and physico-chemical features," on the rationale that this lets it find novel non-conserved but physicochemically ligandable sites ([PRANK, PMC4414931](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4414931/)) **[primary-summarised]** — conservation will systematically penalise cryptic and allosteric pockets. Two gaps bound the claim: **no measurement isolates conservation at top-1 specifically**, and none stratifies it by train/test identity bin or tests it on predicted structures where the MSA exists but the geometry does not. +2.0 Top-(N+2) points is a tie-breaker, not a fix, and is small next to what cascade re-ranking buys (+7 to +14 Top-n points for PRANK over fpocket, three independent confirmations).

A separate line of evidence makes conservation-like sequence signal look even better for the hard cases. On CryptoBench's 222 apo test structures, a **sequence-only ESM2-3B model with no coordinates at all** reaches AUC 0.86–0.88 and AUPRC 0.36–0.43, beating PocketMiner (0.19 AUPRC) and P2Rank on apo input (0.21) ([PMC11725321](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11725321/)) **[verified]**. That evidence is *comparative, not controlled* — no paper runs the ablation that would isolate the sequence contribution — and CryptoSite's own characterisation is that cryptic sites are "as evolutionarily conserved as ordinary pockets, but less hydrophobic and more flexible" **[second-hand]**.

## Chemistry goes on node scalars because nobody has tested anything else

The architecture question has a flat answer: **no paper holds everything else fixed and moves the same chemical information between node scalars, edge features and a parallel chemical graph.** That specific comparison appears genuinely absent from the literature. The de facto convention in every paper that measures anything is **chemistry on node scalars, geometry on edges and in the steerable/tensor channels**. GDEGAN is explicit: node scalars `h_i` are initialised from ESM-2, edge scalars are geometry-only (RBF of distance, smooth cutoff, inner products of degree-`l` steerable features), and spatial information lives in spherical harmonics up to `L_max = 2` **[verified]**. AtomSurf puts chemistry and geometry on *different node sets*: surface vertices carry only geometric descriptors (Gaussian and mean curvature, shape index, normal, heat kernel signature) while the residue graph carries one-hot residue type, secondary structure, hydrophobicity and ESM-650M, with bipartite edges carrying geometry only **[verified]**.

Three design facts are worth extracting. First, **an equivariance cost that the paper states outright**: putting ESM features on node scalars breaks E(3) and leaves only SE(3), because "node features do not encode chirality information" **[verified]**. An achiral per-node chemical descriptor is fine; a PLM embedding silently demotes the guarantee, which is worth an equivariance unit test on whatever gets added. Second, **GDEGAN's multiplicative gating is a third placement option that neither "node" nor "edge" describes**: the message is `m_i = Σ_j (W_a(h_j) ∘ ϕ(r̃_ij) W_rbf ∘ φ(r̃_ij))`, so chemistry sets *what* is sent and geometry sets *how strongly* **[verified]**. The note author's inference: for a model that already has pharmacophore availability on probe nodes, **gating the existing geometric edge terms by the pharmacophore vector rather than concatenating a new channel is the cheapest untested variant**. Third, the field's response to redundancy is **avoidance rather than measurement** — GDEGAN rejects the structure-aware PLM ProstT5 in favour of ESM-2 precisely because it "would create geometric features redundancy," citing AtomSurf, and **never runs either arm** **[verified]**. That makes a measured three-channel null panel with a stated seed sd rarer in this literature than a new architecture.

On **hand-built versus learned**, the comparison the brief wants does not exist. **No controlled, parameter-matched, multi-seed comparison of P2Rank/GRID-style pharmacophore counts and distances against learned atom-type embeddings exists on any protein structure task.** The available substitute is biased in a specific way: in both GDEGAN and VN-EGNN the "learned" arm is **frozen transfer from ESM-2 pre-trained on UniRef**, not something learned end-to-end from the task. GDEGAN's only genuinely end-to-end-learned chemical embedding (atomic number) **loses** to the frozen PLM. So the measured result is *transferred beats hand-built* and *transferred beats learned-from-scratch* — it says nothing about hand-built versus learned-from-scratch at 1,100–25,000 structures. The parameter accounting makes this worse: GDEGAN's column (GotenNet 2.20M, GDEGAN 1.90M, EquiPocket 1.70M) counts **trainable parameters only**, and the frozen 650M-parameter encoder plus its pre-training corpus sit entirely outside the comparison. **AtomSurf is the only genuinely parameter-matched work in the set** (~200k parameters, fixed input features, equal channel widths across hybrid arms, "with the aim of a fair comparison"), and it ablates representations and modules, not chemical features; it reports three replicates as **standard error**, runs no replicates for competitors, and states that "no formal statistical tests were done" **[verified]**. **No paper in this set meets the asking project's own standard.** The corollary cuts in the project's favour: removing a feature also removes parameters, and no paper controls for it, so **a null result is more trustworthy than a positive one**, because the null survives despite also losing capacity.

The small-data failure mode is visible but not quantifiable. DegradoMap (3,260 labelled entries, 28-dim node features, hyperparameters held fixed across arms) reports that removing four of six hand-built feature groups *improved* target-unseen AUROC — lysine indicator +0.101, disorder +0.088, physicochemical +0.074, pLDDT +0.051, "only AA one-hot" +0.055 — concluding that "additional features introduce noise in this small-data regime" ([DegradoMap, arXiv 2606.04021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.04021)) **[verified]**. **But none of those numbers means anything**: the arms are single runs against a 3-seed std of **±0.124** and a 6-seed std of **±0.097**, and the ablation arms ran at a non-tuned learning rate, which the authors flag themselves. It supports the narrative and none of the numbers. The crossover evidence is all from molecular property prediction: on ChEMBL20, Chemprop was beaten by a fingerprint baseline below **~1,024 datapoints** and became competitive above it ([arXiv:2011.12203](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.12203)) **[second-hand]**; a VAE study saw predictive performance plateau around **25K** source molecules while reconstruction kept improving to 250K **[second-hand]**. At 1,100–25,000 structures the asking project straddles exactly that region, which is the regime where hand-built chemistry is competitive or superior and **additional hand-built channels should be expected to add nothing measurable**.

On **which nodes carry chemistry**, only VN-EGNN varies it, and only indirectly. Its physical nodes are residues at Cα carrying the chemistry; **virtual nodes are initialised by averaging residue features across the entire protein**, start on a Fibonacci sphere, connect to residue nodes and not to each other, and "chemical information enters only through the residue nodes" **[verified]**. The closest thing to a which-nodes ablation is heterogeneous versus homogeneous message passing — separate MLPs for physical and virtual nodes — which with ESM on gains COACH420 DCC **0.575(0.008) → 0.605(0.009)**, HOLO4K **0.479 → 0.532**, PDBbind **0.649 → 0.669**, and without ESM is **null to harmful** on three of six metrics (COACH420 DCA 0.700 → 0.684, HOLO4K DCA 0.618 → 0.605) **[verified]**. So probe nodes benefit from their *own transform*, conditional on the protein nodes being rich. **Nothing measures pharmacophore descriptors on probe nodes versus on protein atoms versus on both** — which is exactly the asking project's design, and it has no direct precedent. AtomSurf's surface result deserves one careful reading: parameter-matched at ~200k with fixed inputs, surface-only "consistently falls short" (MSP AuROC 0.500 versus graph 0.609), while the combined model wins everywhere (PIP 0.876, MSP 0.707, PSR R_l 0.452, R_g 0.831) **[verified]**. The note author's inference for the project's −0.011 surface null: the gap is best explained by *how* the surface is coupled, not by whether surfaces help. AtomSurf's gain comes from **bipartite feature sharing at every layer** (16 nearest graph nodes per vertex, distance and normal angle in 16 RBFs) plus genuinely surface-specific descriptors the atom graph cannot compute; a late-fusion module that sees the same geometry the main graph already sees is the configuration AtomSurf's own "sequential is underwhelming" finding predicts will fail. Before declaring the surface channel dead, test curvature/shape-index/HKS descriptors and per-layer bipartite coupling.

The **input/output redundancy** question — seven protein pharmacophore-availability input channels against seven complementary ligand-atom output classes — has essentially no literature. The notes found no structural-model study of input/output channel correspondence in either direction. The one on-point precedent is an INTERSPEECH 2019 ASR paper that adds an auxiliary branch reconstructing the input features and addresses the identity-copy risk by **deliberately distorting the input in the auxiliary branch only** ([IBM Research](https://research.ibm.com/publications/multi-task-ctc-training-with-auxiliary-feature-reconstruction-for-end-to-end-speech-recognition)) **[second-hand, a stated design choice, not a measured comparison]** — which at least establishes that practitioners elsewhere treat the hazard as live. Because a protein donor implies a ligand acceptor, the input→output map is close to a **fixed permutation plus a geometric availability mask**, making a near-identity solution reachable. Two diagnostics need no literature at all: train the hotspot head on the pharmacophore input alone with geometric channels removed or randomised, and read the resulting AUROC as the **echo floor**; and permute the seven input channels at evaluation and check whether the seven output channels permute with them. Because the primary metric is site ranking rather than hotspot AP, the overlap is more likely a **dilution** risk than a correctness risk, and GDEGAN gives the relevant measured pattern: its auxiliary directional loss buys **+0.031** COACH420 DCC when the main input is weak, **+0.013** once ESM is present, and **+0.008 against the arm's own 0.008 std** in the strongest configuration — plus one sign reversal (HOLO4K DCA 0.753(0.004) → 0.749(0.006)) that the paper's "+2% DCC / 3.5% DCA" headline averages over **[verified]**. The transferable conclusion is that **auxiliary heads buy most where the primary representation is impoverished and approximately nothing once it is strong**, so the cost of many heads is mostly opportunity cost, not primary-metric damage. Gradient surgery is not the answer either: **no PCGrad/GradNorm/uncertainty-weighting result exists on any protein structure task**, and MatSciML's null — "PCGrad provides little performance improvement compared to multi-task learning with additive losses," attributed to tasks being highly correlated and so offering few gradient conflicts ([arXiv:2309.05934](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.05934)) **[second-hand]** — is the most relevant warning for seven correlated pharmacophore heads.

There is **no accepted name** for the phenomenon the project measured. The nearest defensible framings, in descending order of support, are HonestAffinity's **split-conditioned reversal** (which has numbers and a mechanism), generic **feature redundancy** (widely used, undefined), and **negative transfer** (multi-task-specific, a poor fit for an input-channel ablation). The only source anywhere that defines a redundancy *metric* reports "pairwise combinatorial ablation of same-pathway features produces universally subadditive effects (median **redundancy ratio 0.74**)" in a single-cell foundation model ([arXiv 2603.11940](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11940)) **[second-hand]**. Two further absences should be stated rather than hedged: **no paper reports that adding partial charges does nothing because atom types are already embedded** (the notes searched for exactly this pattern), and **no published analogue exists for the degree-2 steerable-tensor null**. The nearest bracketing evidence comes from outside the field — NequIP's capacity-matched l=0 arm, where "the equivariant networks with l ∈ 1, 2, 3 significantly outperform the invariant networks with l = 0 for all data set sizes," but whose l=0 arm is SchNet-style and therefore also removes angular input (**capacity-matched, geometry-unmatched**) ([NequIP, arXiv:2101.03164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03164)) **[verified]**; and "Beyond Canonicalization," whose 2×2 scalarises the *same* local-frame geometry and still loses 0.04–0.05 cosine similarity on normal-vector regression (**geometry-matched, capacity-unmatched**), while reporting only +0.9 mIoU on ShapeNet part segmentation where "geometric information … may be less important" ([arXiv:2405.15389](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15389)) **[verified]**. NequIP also localises the payoff at l=0→1: "further increasing the rotation order l beyond l = 1 again only shifts the learning curve and does not result in an additional change in log-log slope." And EquiformerV2 shows the sign of an `L_max` increase is architecture-conditional — the same 6→8 change degrades eSCN's energy MAE and improves EquiformerV2's **[primary, automated table extraction]**. **A degree-2 null on a protein pocket task is consistent with all of this.**

## A pharmacophore map is table stakes; its evaluation is the actual opening

Spatially resolved, chemistry-typed interaction fields are a forty-year-old idea, and as of 2026 at least three code-released deep models already emit them. **PharmRL** produces a dense multi-label field of six pharmacophore types (HBA, HBD, hydrophobic, aromatic, negative ion, positive ion) at **0.5 Å** over a 9.5 Å cube, trained on 157,252 points from PDBbind v2019 with ligand-Tanimoto-aware CV folds, reporting **per-class ROC-AUC 0.951–0.982** ([PharmRL, PMC11687028](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11687028/)) **[verified]**. **PharmacoNet** uses a 3D Swin Transformer V2 FPN on 64³ voxels at 0.5 Å and emits discrete points with centre, radius and type, scored by an **analytical function with seven hand-set weights (1.0/4.0/8.0) and no learned parameters** ([PharmacoNet, PMC11575537](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11575537/)) **[verified]**. **PharmacoForge** is a variance-preserving equivariant diffusion model over GVP-GNN layers generating sets of typed centres conditioned on the pocket ([PharmacoForge, PMC12451294](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12451294/)) **[verified]**. So "a deep model that outputs a pharmacophore map" is **not by itself a contribution**. Licence status is uniformly weak: **none of the three papers names a software licence** (PharmRL's article is CC BY 4.0, which covers the article and not the code), PharmRL and PharmacoForge **decline to say whether weights ship**, and PharmacoNet's trained models are on Zenodo. The classical side is worse for reproducibility: the CCDC Hotspots API is **MIT-licensed but depends on the commercial CSD Python API** plus a separately compiled GHECOM binary ([github.com/prcurran/hotspots](https://github.com/prcurran/hotspots)) **[verified]**, so Fragment Hotspot Maps is **not reproducibly runnable by an unlicensed academic**; FMOPhore is GPL + CC-BY-4.0 but needs Glide SP and GAMESS **[verified]**; FTMap is a web service whose landing page **404'd** in the research session **[verified, the 404 itself]**.

What is genuinely unoccupied is the evaluation. There is **no community benchmark for pharmacophore-map accuracy**. Two protocol families exist. Family A compares against the holo ligand's own typed atoms: **SuperStar 2001** is the only classical method with a population-scale, type-resolved validation — propensity maps for all binding sites of **122 X-ray complexes**, metric = mean distance from each ligand atom to the nearest same-type peak, **1.0–1.2 Å** for the three H-bonding probes (**0.8–1.0 Å** for solvent-inaccessible atoms) and **~1.5 Å** for methyl carbon, plus **24 metal complexes** where coordination geometry was derived correctly in ~75% of cases ([SuperStar, PMID 11273705](https://europepmc.org/abstract/MED/11273705)) **[verified]**. Fragment Hotspot Maps, the method closest in intent, is validated on **21 fragments** with a **median percentage rank of 97%** plus two case studies ([Radoux et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jmedchem.5b01980)) **[verified]**; a thesis splits that as 98% for fragment atoms versus **72% for lead atoms** **[second-hand]**. Family B is downstream screening enrichment, and it is what the 2024–2026 deep models actually report. **That is the benchmark a new method will be measured against whether or not it is the right one: LIT-PCBA EF/F1 via Pharmit, head-to-head with PharmRL, PharmacoNet and Apo2ph4.** PharmacoForge wins best F1 on 12 of 18 LIT-PCBA systems and best EF on 13, versus PharmRL 5/4 and Apo2ph4 1/1 **[verified]**.

Two primary-source results say the same uncomfortable thing about dense fields. PharmRL's own naive top-3/4/5 CNN-ranked feature combinations gave **F1 = 0 in all but one case (F1 = 0.028)**, which is why the authors bolted a deep geometric reinforcement-learning *selector* on top **[verified]**; and **40% of PharmacoForge's generated queries returned no hits at all** **[verified]**. **The hard part is turning a field into a usable query, not producing the field.** On utility, the honest summary is that a map buys throughput and interpretability and roughly docking-parity enrichment, not accuracy over docking: PharmacoNet "outperformed AutoDock Vina and was close to Smina" on DEKOIS 2.0, at **0.39 ms/molecule** (1 conformer) and **2.60 ms** (128 conformers) on a 32-core CPU, screening **187M ZINC20 molecules against CB2 in ~20.1 h**, with reported speedups of **3,956×/3,483× over Vina and 34,117×/27,731× over GLIDE SP** **[verified]**. Also notable: **feature directionality is not predicted by any of the three** (PharmacoForge says so explicitly), which is an opening an equivariant model is naturally suited to. And **no per-voxel calibration (ECE) or per-type confusion matrix is reported anywhere** in the four papers read. The 0.95–0.98 AUROC figure carries its own caveat the notes insist on: PharmRL's negatives are partly adversarial and hand-constructed (points clashing with protein atoms, features too far from a complementary group), so that number is **not evidence of chemical discrimination at the hard decision boundary**.

The deepest weakness in all prior art is the **label**: every Family-A reference standard is derived from a single bound ligand (Pharmit features, PLIP interactions, holo atom types), and one ligand does not probe a whole pocket — PharmRL's authors say so, and two of their eight DUD-E targets *exceeded* the ligand-feature ceiling **[verified]**. Moving the label to many fragments on one protein is the scientifically correct move, and the published map-versus-screen comparisons are sobering: in PurC, "all fragments from this standard difference-map screen occupy positions the hotspot mapping predicted" and almost 60% of PanDDA hits occupy hotspot 1 ([PMC6501894](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6501894/)) **[verified]**, while in *S. mansoni* thioredoxin glutathione reductase **only 2 of 16 experimentally observed fragment sites corresponded to FTMap hotspots** ([Sci Rep 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-52018-2)) **[second-hand]**. That disagreement suggests classical maps have poor recall on fragment sites outside the primary pocket, which is a measurable edge for a model that can recover secondary and allosteric ones. Two confounds must be handled up front or the benchmark will be attacked: **PanDDA's low-occupancy sensitivity** (the PurC authors themselves doubt some of their own hits are real hotspots, and note occupancies of ~0.4–1.0 "may not support elaboration to adequate affinity"), and **library composition bias** — the Poised/DSI libraries are not uniform over pharmacophore types, so a per-type hit rate must be normalised by the library's own composition or it will just recover the library. The closest ready-made resource is a 10-target benchmark of **309 protein–fragment complexes from 225 unique fragments, 13–65 fragment-bound structures per target** ([PMC9421645](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9421645/)) **[second-hand]**, and the live Fragalysis inventory could not be read in the research session, so **no current count or data licence should be printed**.

For interoperability, the only verified open-world output contract is CCDC Hotspots': `HotspotWriter` emits an `out.zip` of **`.grd` grid files plus a PyMOL run script**, with scored molecules written as mol2 **[verified]**; the deep papers visualise in PyMOL and screen in Pharmit, making **the Pharmit query the de facto interchange format** **[verified]**. Commercial vendors — SiteMap, SZMAP, Cresset — **publish no export formats at all**, which is itself a finding and a legitimate differentiator for an open tool. The claim that CCP4/MRC/DX load across PyMOL, ChimeraX, Maestro and MOE is **unverified domain knowledge from the notes, not a verified fact**, and GRID's energy terms, probe library, licence and `.kont` format, AutoGrid's `.map` format, and SiteMap's Dscore coefficients and validation-set size are all **unverified** and must not be asserted.

## What to add next, ranked — and what the project's own nulls already rule out

The asking project ranks pockets with per-probe pharmacophore availability features already in hand, and has measured a seed sd of **0.009**, which puts its detectable effect size at roughly **±0.02 at a few seeds**. Most published feature-ablation deltas in this entire literature are smaller than their own seed spread. The binding constraint on the next decision is therefore **statistical power, not feature design** — the cheapest way to make "does channel X help?" answerable is more seeds, not more channels.

### Tier 3 — the project's own nulls predict these will also be null

Three measured nulls at **−0.010 (frozen ESM-2 650M)**, **−0.011 (SAS surface module)** and **−0.006 (degree-2 tensor channels)** against a seed sd of **0.009** are a normal, predicted result, not an anomaly — and two of the three have close published analogues. They collectively predict nulls for the following, and each should be skipped or demoted unless a specific mechanism argues otherwise.

**Any further frozen or structure-aware protein language model channel (ProstT5 included).** HonestAffinity's ΔR changes sign between canonical and leak-proof splits (**+0.019 val and +0.034 CASF for ESM, but −0.024/−0.042/−0.064 on test_cl1/cl2/cl3**), AtomSurf's ESM drop **shrinks on PINDER's stricter splits**, and GDEGAN rejects ProstT5 a priori on redundancy grounds without running either arm. Under a similarity-filtered evaluation the project's −0.010 **is** the expected value. Note also HonestAffinity's own honesty about variance: its NoESM arm's std devs grow to **0.033/0.059/0.076**, so its headline +0.064 is within one std dev of that arm.

**Partial charges as an extra float channel.** They are nearly collinear with atom-type one-hots — an O is negative, an N–H positive — which is exactly what dMaSIF's **r = 0.83** reconstruction of PB from types plus distances demonstrates quantitatively. Pafnucy, Kalasanty and DeepSurf carry the channel and **never measured it**; P2Rank's charge features are its **least** important (vsCation 0.00083, posCharge 0.0010, hBondDonor 0.0011 against protrusion 0.0845); OnionNet explicitly refuses charges and stays competitive, arguing that AM1-BCC charges are computed "without considering the solvent environment and dipole moments" ([OnionNet, arXiv:1906.02418](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.02418)) **[second-hand]**. The one number that could change this verdict — SwinSite's SI partial-charge arm — **remains unretrieved**.

**An explicit PB/APBS or generalized-Born input grid.** dMaSIF is a direct controlled refutation on a site task, the feature step costs **19.69 ± 16.08 s/protein** (≈120× MaSIF's own forward pass, ≈550× dMaSIF's, by the notes' arithmetic on verified numbers), the cached features exceed **1 TB**, and a JCTC concern holds that even the standard **0.5 Å** grid spacing "may not give reliable binding free energies with APBS, DelPhi, and PBSA" **[second-hand]**. **No model anywhere feeds a GB potential as an input grid** — an absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.

**Atomic multipole inputs.** The one ablation-shaped result goes the wrong way (charges + dipoles + quadrupoles **worse** and more overfit than charges + quadrupoles), and computing a protein's atomic multipoles requires QM or a polarizable force field — a preprocessing cost well above a PB solve. **No controlled ablation of multipole input features exists in any protein–ligand model.**

**Learned or equilibrated charges (QEq, EEM, learned electronegativity, auxiliary charge heads).** **No downstream structural-task evidence exists at all** — the clearest negative finding in the notes, to be reported as such rather than hedged. The physical condition where QEq wins (global non-local charge redistribution) has no analogue in local site detection. Their real value is engineering, not accuracy: an EspalomaCharge-style assigner removes the AM1-BCC bottleneck for ligands and non-standard residues, and nobody has measured that end-to-end on a site benchmark.

**More hand-built pharmacophore or physicochemical channels in general.** At 1,100–25,000 structures the project sits in the regime where this literature predicts hand-built chemistry is already competitive and additional channels add nothing measurable; DegradoMap is what the failure mode looks like in practice (plausible channels, collectively indistinguishable from noise at ±0.124). **No controlled, parameter-matched, multi-seed comparison of pharmacophore counts against learned embeddings exists**, so there is no positive evidence to override the prior.

**Further degree-2 or higher steerable channels.** NequIP localises the payoff at l=0→1 ("further increasing the rotation order l beyond l = 1 … only shifts the learning curve and does not result in an additional change in log-log slope"), "Beyond Canonicalization" finds the scalarisation penalty small where characteristic directions matter less (**+0.9 mIoU** on segmentation versus +0.05 cosine on normal regression), and EquiformerV2 shows the sign of an `L_max` increase is architecture-conditional. **No binding-site paper has ever swept `l_max`** — GDEGAN fixes it at 2 and its hyperparameter table lists the single value 2 — so the project's −0.006 is the field's first datapoint, and it is a publishable one.

**Gradient surgery (PCGrad, GradNorm, uncertainty weighting) across the hotspot heads.** No measured case exists on any protein structure task, and MatSciML's null says gradient surgery has few conflicts to resolve when the tasks are highly correlated — which seven complementary pharmacophore classes plus a site score almost certainly are. At sd 0.009, detecting such an effect would need more seeds than it is worth. Measure the simple thing first: does dropping heads move the primary metric beyond ±0.009 across enough seeds?

**Additional auxiliary heads on top of an already-strong primary representation.** GDEGAN's directional loss buys **+0.031** when the input is weak and **+0.008 against its own 0.008 std** in the strongest configuration, with one measured sign reversal that the paper's average conceals. EquiPocket's equivalent direction loss helps small proteins (0–1000 atoms **0.319 → 0.428**) and is harmful at 4000–5000 atoms (**0.258 → 0.255**) **[verified, no variance reported]**.

Two diagnostics belong before any of this, because they cost nothing and the literature cannot answer them. Run the **echo floor** test — train the hotspot head on the pharmacophore input alone with the geometric channels removed or randomised, and read whatever AUROC/AP that degenerate model reaches as the floor; if the full model's hotspot metric sits near it, the head is echoing the input permutation rather than doing geometry. And compute the **base rates** nobody has published: the fraction of true binding cavities containing ≥1 ordered water versus the fraction of decoy cavities that do, which is the measurement the whole unhappy-water hypothesis rests on and which the notes judge computable in an afternoon from any standard benchmark.

## Conclusion

The pattern across all seven priority topics is the same, and it is not the one the chemistry literature implies: **every chemical representation that requires a solve has been beaten by a learned function of atom identity and distance, and every chemical representation that is already implicit in atom types adds nothing measurable on top of them.** The two exceptions are informative because they are not really chemistry — evolutionary information, which carries family-level functional priors that geometry cannot see, and metal presence, which is a hard atom the model was simply not given. That reframes the design question. The useful axis is not "how much physics can we afford" but "what information is genuinely absent from atom types plus coordinates," and the honest answer at pocket-ranking scale is: an alignment, a metal, and a confidence on each.

The second conclusion is methodological and is the one worth acting on. The asking project's three nulls are more informative than most published positive results in this field, because this literature's default response to suspected redundancy is **avoidance rather than measurement** — GDEGAN rejects ProstT5 on redundancy grounds and runs neither arm; Kalasanty and DeepSurf ship an unexamined partial charge inherited from an affinity model; no binding-site paper has ever swept `l_max` or ablated a metal channel or a protonation state. Judged by the project's own standard (parameter-matched arms, stated seed counts, effects larger than seed spread), only AtomSurf qualifies, and it does not ablate chemical features. A three-channel null panel with a stated seed sd of 0.009, reported alongside a HOLO4K-separate breakdown and a paired per-protein test, is a rarer and more durable contribution than another equivariant backbone — and it also licenses the sharper claim the field cannot currently make: that at 1,100–25,000 structures with similarity-filtered evaluation, the binding constraint on chemical feature design is statistical power, and the correct next investment is seeds, stratification and an MSA, not another channel.
